\section{Trust: what exactly is being checked?}
\label{sec:trust}

\subsection{Three layers that are not interchangeable}

It is essential to keep apart the mathematical soundness of a certificate
format, the software correctness of a checker, and a proof accepted by Lean.
The propositions in this document explain why each certificate implies the
statement it claims. Tests provide evidence about the Python implementations.
Neither supplies a Lean theorem. A completed tactic must either reconstruct a
Lean proof or use a formally verified checker whose result is established
inside Lean.

\begin{proposition}[Certificate-format soundness is not implementation
correctness]
Let $\mathcal{C}$ be a certificate format with a proved soundness statement
``$\mathrm{check}(P,C)=\texttt{true}\Rightarrow P$ holds'', and let $K$ be a
program intended to compute $\mathrm{check}$. Evidence that $K$ accepts only
certificates satisfying $\mathrm{check}$ is evidence about $K$, obtained by
testing. It does not follow from the soundness statement, and the soundness
statement does not follow from it.
\end{proposition}

This is laboured deliberately, because the gap is where an automation project
most easily deceives itself. The prototypes here replay every stored
certificate with the standard library alone, and several run their checkers in
a direction algorithmically opposite to their producers --- a Bernstein checker
that \emph{expands} a claimed basis representation and compares it against an
independently computed affine transform shares no conversion formula with the
search that proposed it. That is real, and it is still not kernel verification.

\subsection{The acceptance judgement}

An accepted source theorem must have:

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.4em]
\item no unresolved expression or universe metavariables;
\item no \code{sorry} dependency anywhere in its transitive closure;
\item no unexpected new axioms relative to the approved environment;
\item every guard and side condition discharged, not merely recorded;
\item a proof of \emph{the exact original goal}, not of a restated one.
\end{enumerate}

The last point deserves emphasis because it is the one a certificate pipeline
gets wrong most easily.

\begin{invariant}[Certificate binding]
\label{inv:binding}
A certificate is checked against the problem derived from the \emph{source}
--- the same variable mapping, types, assumptions, and target. The checker
receives the original target and hypotheses externally. It must never accept a
problem statement supplied by the certificate itself. A certificate for
another polynomial, another box, or a different quantifier scope is not
interchangeable with the one required.
\end{invariant}

A correct polynomial identity about the wrong reification is not a proof of
the user's goal. A certificate must identify the actual hypotheses it used,
not merely provide polynomial data that resembles them. The prototypes test
this directly: removing a necessary constraint from the problem makes replay
fail, and a certificate re-attached to a changed target is rejected.

\begin{remark}[A type is not a check]
An \code{Expr} field is not a proof-validation boundary. Calling a structure
\code{CheckedProof} does not make its contents checked, and a reported
dependency list is a diagnostic: the acceptance layer must recompute
dependencies from the accepted term. The Lean design contract in this collection
deliberately names a submitted term \code{Candidate} for exactly this reason.
Likewise, a successful \code{inferType} or unification inside tactic
metaprogramming is part of elaboration, not a replacement for the final kernel
boundary --- an expression can type-check because an imported axiom states the
desired result.
\end{remark}

\subsection{A status lattice: ten outcomes, and what each licenses}
\label{sec:lattice}

A binary split between ``certificate accepted'' and ``no certificate''
under-specifies this system badly. Every real integration lives in the middle,
and the middle is where a design silently upgrades a heuristic into evidence.
The remedy is to enumerate the outcomes and attach to each one the consequence
it \emph{permits} --- so that permission is a property of the outcome rather
than a decision the consuming code makes locally.

\begin{longtable}{@{}>{\raggedright\arraybackslash}p{0.23\textwidth}>{\raggedright\arraybackslash}p{0.24\textwidth}>{\raggedright\arraybackslash}p{0.47\textwidth}@{}}
\toprule
Outcome & Typical producer & Permitted consequence \\
\midrule
\endhead

reifier, fragment classifier &
Retain the goal with the refusal class. Never a negative result about the
goal. \\

budget, policy, inapplicable domain &
Retain. Carries no information about the goal at all. \\
Heuristic status &
raw solver \code{sat} / \code{unsat} / \code{unknown}; a learned score &

Candidate proposed &
any untrusted search &
Enter the obligation graph as a candidate. Never the fact store. \\

solver model, sampled point &
None yet. Decode and replay it before it means anything. \\

\bottomrule
\end{longtable}

Three rules govern movement through this lattice.

\begin{invariant}[Evidence is attached to a tuple, not a name]
\label{inv:tuple}
A claim is attached to the exact tuple of domain, inventory, encoding,
candidate, hypotheses and policy that produced it. No cache key, diagnostic
name, rendered term, hash, model, or solver session may stand in for that tuple
unless the substitution has itself been checked.
\end{invariant}

\begin{invariant}[Guidance and authority are separate]
Fingerprints, sample signatures, solver scores, learned clauses and unsat cores
may direct enumeration without becoming evidence. Evidence requires the
independent validation path; a proof-grade claim additionally requires the
declared proof authority.
\end{invariant}

\begin{invariant}[Pruning states its approximation polarity]
\label{inv:polarity}
An \emph{over}-approximation of the remaining search space may justify pruning
--- when even the over-approximation is unsatisfiable, nothing was lost. An
\emph{under}-approximation may produce witnesses but may \emph{never} justify
pruning. An exact encoding may do either. Any translation that drops a
distinction the domain cares about must degrade to \emph{inapplicable} or
\emph{unknown}: it must not silently strengthen a precondition, weaken a
postcondition, replace a total function by a partial one, equate distinct
nominal families, erase overflow, or assume termination.
\end{invariant}

Invariant~\ref{inv:polarity} is the safety argument for any learned or
numerical pruning this design might later acquire, and it is the one most
easily lost when a heuristic is described as ``just a filter''.

\begin{remark}[Caches and fingerprints]
Two corollaries of Invariant~\ref{inv:tuple} are worth stating because they are
cheap to get wrong. A reusable artefact should be reduced to the minimal
\emph{re-checkable input}, and every reuse must re-run the check --- a store
holding inputs is a search heuristic and is safe, whereas a store holding
verdicts is a cache and is not. And a fingerprint that keys a cached result
must include the solver version, invocation profile, parameters, encoding
version and objective order; a fingerprint over the problem alone is unsound
across a solver upgrade.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Kernel-only and native-checked are different profiles}

The Lean kernel verifies derivations relative to the axioms they use, so a
strict automation policy must inspect the transitive axiom dependencies of the
final proof and of its auxiliary declarations. A practical whitelist may allow
the project's usual logical axioms --- propositional extensionality, quotient
soundness, and classical choice where permitted --- while rejecting admitted
proofs and compiler-result axioms.

The current tactic reference documents that \code{native\_decide} and
\code{bv\_decide} rely on native code generation and associated trusted
results~\cite{tactic-reference,validating-proofs}. Consequently ``the SAT
solver returns a checked certificate'' does not by itself mean ``no compiler
trust is added''. \forge{} should place that route behind an opt-in trust mode
rather than enabling it under a kernel-only label, and the active profile must
be visible in the trace and in the output metadata.

Two further details matter for anyone implementing the audit. Native
evaluation in recent Lean versions can introduce a \emph{separate generated
axiom per computation}, so a single-name blacklist such as ``reject
\code{Lean.trustCompiler}'' is not a sufficient
policy~\cite{native-infra,native-rfc}. And the audit must follow the actual
pinned Lean version rather than a remembered one; the set of axioms a given
tactic can introduce is a property of the release.

A kernel-only bit-vector route would require replaying a supported certificate
checker through a reduction mechanism that introduces no native-result axioms,
and measuring the resulting cost. That route is proposed here, not implemented.
Lean4Lean is the relevant reference point for what independent kernel-level
assurance would mean~\cite{lean4lean}.

\subsection{Two ways to accept a proof the kernel never checked}
\label{sec:kernel-traps}

The acceptance judgement above is not self-executing. In the pinned Lean
v4.34.0 source there are two concrete paths by which an implementation that
believes it is kernel-checking is not. Both were verified directly against the
installed toolchain rather than inferred, and neither appears in any of the
nine source proposals.

\paragraph{An inherited option can switch the kernel off.}
\code{Lean.Kernel.Environment.addDecl} branches on a builtin option:

\begin{lstlisting}
def Kernel.Environment.addDecl (env : Environment) (opts : Options)
    (decl : Declaration) (cancelTk? : Option IO.CancelToken := none)
    : Except Exception Environment :=
  if debug.skipKernelTC.get opts then
    addDeclWithoutChecking env decl
  else
    addDeclCore env ... decl cancelTk?
\end{lstlisting}

The option's own registration carries the warning: \emph{``skip kernel type
checker. WARNING: setting this option to true may compromise soundness because
your proofs will not be checked by the Lean kernel.''} Its default is
\code{false}, but the value used is whichever \code{Options} the caller passes
--- so a verifier that inherits ambient options inherits whatever the
surrounding file, project, or an enclosing tactic set. A certificate pipeline
must \emph{pin the option off} in the options it passes, not merely refrain
from setting it.

This is not hypothetical for the Boolean lane of \S\ref{sec:boolean}. The
v4.34.0 documentation for \code{bv\_decide} lists the same option as a
supported knob: it ``may be set to true to disable actually checking the LRAT
proof'', leaving a run that ``still runs bitblasting + SAT solving, so this
option essentially trusts the SAT solver''. A benchmark that did not pin the
option would be comparing two different trust profiles while reporting one.

\paragraph{The ordinary declaration path is asynchronous.}
\code{Lean.addDecl} in \code{CoreM} goes through \code{env.addConstAsync},
commits \emph{preliminary} constant information immediately, and --- when
\code{Elab.async} is set --- defers the actual check to a task:

\begin{lstlisting}
let async <- env.addConstAsync (reportExts := false) name kind ...
async.commitConst async.asyncEnv (some info) ...     -- reported immediately
...
if Elab.async.get (<- getOptions) then
  let t <- BaseIO.mapTask checkAct env.checked        -- checked later
  Core.logSnapshotTask { ... task := t, ... }
\end{lstlisting}

So the declaration becomes visible before the kernel has accepted it.
An acceptance step that adds a declaration and then observes that it is present
has observed nothing. The acceptance decision must either call the synchronous
\code{Kernel.Environment.addDecl} directly, or await the environment's checked
task before answering.

\begin{invariant}[Synchronous, option-pinned checking]
\label{inv:sync}
Acceptance calls the synchronous kernel entry point with
\code{debug.skipKernelTC} explicitly set to \code{false} in the options it
passes, and does not report success on the basis of a declaration's visibility.
\end{invariant}

\paragraph{A stamp that cannot be forged.}
The corresponding discipline in the type is that the evidence of checking must
be constructible only by the checker. A suitable shape is an opaque stamp
returned by the verifier, binding the candidate, the expected type, the
environment and toolchain identity, and the options actually used. A
fingerprint may be a \emph{view} of that association; it must not be a
constructor for it. A \code{Bool} field named \code{kernelChecked} that any
caller can set to \code{true} is the same mistake as the \code{CheckedProof}
structure of the previous remark, one layer down.

A workable verification pipeline, then, is: instantiate all assigned
metavariables and reject any remaining expression or universe metavariables;
check that every free variable belongs to the captured local context and that
no local declaration has escaped its scope; abstract the local context into a
closed type and value, preserving binder information and dependency order;
build a fresh private declaration; call the synchronous kernel checker on a
scratch environment with the option pinned; discard that environment unless the
result is committed; and delaborate independently for display, optionally
re-elaborating the displayed text to establish that what the user is shown is
what was checked.

\subsection{What a working verification boundary looks like}
\label{sec:boundary-impl}

The preceding subsections say what acceptance must establish. None of the nine
proposals built it. A neighbouring project has, for term synthesis rather than
for certificates, and its shape is worth recording because several of its
decisions are not the obvious ones.

\paragraph{Re-elaborate the original goal text, every time.}
The candidate is checked by sending Lean a program of the form
\begin{lstlisting}
noncomputable example : (GOAL) := (TERM)
\end{lstlisting}
where \code{GOAL} is the user's original text, parenthesised, re-parsed and
re-elaborated on every single candidate. There is no ``reuse the already
elaborated goal expression'' shortcut. That is crude, and it is exactly the
invariant of \S\ref{sec:trust}: a proof of the \emph{exact original goal}
rather than of a restatement, made unforgeable by never holding on to the
restatement.

Two modifiers carry weight. \code{example} rather than \code{def} makes the
check \emph{ephemeral}: it adds no declaration, so neither success nor failure
mutates the session. \code{noncomputable} disables executable-code generation
without weakening elaboration, universe checking, safety checking, or the
kernel type check --- so a candidate is not rejected for a compilation failure
that is not a type error.

One modifier is a mistake worth not copying: \code{autoImplicit} must be
\emph{off} at a certificate boundary. With it on, an unbound identifier in the
goal --- a typo, or a name that has fallen out of scope --- is silently
generalised into an implicit parameter, so the candidate is verified against a
generalised goal rather than the one that was asked.

\paragraph{Committed environment as a positive requirement.}
The natural formulation of acceptance is ``no errors and no \code{sorry}''.
That is not enough, and the gap is easy to miss: a response carrying no error
diagnostics, no \code{sorry} obligations, \emph{and no environment} means the
elaborator never committed a checked command at all. The reference
implementation checks for a committed environment on three of its four
verification paths and omits it on the fourth --- which is precisely the
``looks well-typed'' hole this section warns about, surviving in production
code that is otherwise careful.

\begin{invariant}[Positive evidence of checking]
\label{inv:positive}
Acceptance requires a positive signal that the checker ran and committed ---
not merely the absence of complaints.
\end{invariant}

\paragraph{A closed rejection vocabulary with a documented precedence.}
Failure classification should be an enumeration, not a string, and its order
should be fixed: transport failure, then a protocol-level fatal response, then
an error diagnostic, then a remaining \code{sorry}. Two consequences follow.
Transport failure is \emph{not} a semantic failure --- Lean rejecting a term is
a healthy outcome that belongs to the semantic classifier, while a dead
subprocess belongs to the liveness layer, and conflating them is how a timeout
becomes a refutation. And infrastructure degradation must \emph{void} results
computed under it: if the channel failed at any point during a batch, a
successful-looking result from that batch is converted to a failure when the
scope closes, even if no individual step noticed.

\paragraph{The \code{sorry} check is structural, not textual.}
A protocol that reports outstanding proof obligations as data lets the checker
reject on that data rather than by grepping the source, which catches
\code{sorry} introduced by a tactic or by elaboration error recovery, not only
\code{sorry} the user typed.

\paragraph{Two tiers, because the fast gate cannot afford the slow check.}
The in-session gate --- errors, obligations, committed environment --- costs on
the order of a hundred to a few hundred milliseconds per candidate. A
transitive axiom audit is far more expensive and does not belong in that loop.
The workable split is a \emph{fast} per-candidate gate in the session, and a
\emph{slow} out-of-session gate that replays each accepted result in a fresh
process, in an environment containing only what that result needs, and requires
an empty axiom inventory. The fast gate filters; the slow gate is what an
assurance claim rests on.

That cost figure has an architectural consequence, not merely an engineering
one. If verification dominates, then verifying lazily --- best candidate first,
stopping at a quota, never forcing the tail --- is the design, not an
optimisation. It is also why the scheduler of \S\ref{sec:architecture} needs a
verification budget distinct from its search budget: each failed reconstruction
costs real time, and a worker that proposes many cheap candidates can exhaust
the checker while a worker that proposes one expensive one never runs.

\paragraph{Make the receipt unforgeable in the type system.}
\S\ref{sec:trust} observed that naming a structure \code{CheckedProof} does not
make its contents checked. The constructive version of that remark is an
abstract type whose constructor is not exported, produced only by the function
that actually calls the checker:

\begin{lstlisting}
-- Opaque receipt that the verification callback accepted a candidate.
-- This records only callback acceptance at this boundary. It is NOT
-- behavioural evidence, a solver certificate, or a kernel proof. A caller
-- needing a stronger claim must retain and replay that authority separately.
opaque Verified (candidate : Type) : Type
\end{lstlisting}

Three properties make this an enforced boundary rather than a convention: the
constructor is module-private, so nothing downstream can mint one; the type
parameter is nominal, so no coercion can forge one across representationally
equal types; and the documentation deliberately \emph{under}-claims, fixing a
ceiling that everything downstream must respect.

\paragraph{Ranking is a capability too.}
An overlooked hole: the boundary usually governs \emph{admission} and leaves
\emph{presentation} ungoverned. But a ranker that may reorder or filter a set
of checked results can drop one, duplicate one, or --- worse --- reassociate a
result with another's evidence. The fix is to hand the ranker opaque,
epoch-scoped handles and validate that what comes back is a total, injective
map into what went out, checking collection limits, length, index range and
duplication, and \emph{reconstructing the output from the original receipts at
the proposed indices} rather than from anything the ranker returned. Any seal
failure falls back atomically to the original batch in its original order.

The same discipline answers a question \S\ref{sec:search-ir} raises: identity
for selection purposes must be a private index, not payload equality, because
equal payloads are still different occurrences and only one of them may carry
the authority.

\subsection{Diagnostic holes are not accepted proofs}

The inspected \grind{} configuration includes an option concerning admitted
branches in trace-oriented diagnostics~\cite{grind-config}. That is not a claim
that ordinary successful \grind{} proofs are unsound. It is a reminder that a
coordinator must never mistake a diagnostic artefact for a finished proof: the
root declaration's dependencies must be checked for admitted terms even when an
inner tool reports a successful diagnostic operation.

Absence of the token \code{sorry} in a source file is likewise not evidence
that the file compiles, and it is not evidence that the declarations it
contains are proved. A benchmark harness should detect an admitted declaration
in a tactic's output and record it as a rejection rather than a success.

\subsection{Soundness invariants to assert in code}

\begin{invariant}[Context validity]
Every proved fact is accompanied by a proof whose free variables and local
instances belong to its recorded context, or by an explicit abstraction and
instantiation that transports it into the consuming context.
\end{invariant}

\begin{invariant}[Candidate quarantine]
No candidate theorem, model equation, numerical conjecture, or unsolved proof
hole is admitted to the proved-fact state. Exploratory assumptions never serve
as proof evidence.
\end{invariant}

\begin{invariant}[Induction discipline]
An induction hypothesis is used only with the scope and smaller-argument
relationship supplied by its recursor. Generalised parameters may vary; the
structural induction index may not be replaced arbitrarily.
\end{invariant}

\begin{invariant}[Fail-closed arithmetic]
Numerical tolerances may guide search. Accepted equalities, coefficient signs,
partition boundaries, and proof-rule premises are checked exactly.
\end{invariant}

\begin{invariant}[Acyclic justification]
A lemma is not available while its own certificate is being checked, and a
bundle of lemmas is accepted only in a dependency order in which no entry
refers to a later one. Name shadowing of a defining equation or of the
induction hypothesis is rejected outright.
\end{invariant}

Invariant~\ref{inv:binding} completes the list. Each of these has an
executable analogue in at least one prototype, and the corresponding negative
tests are part of the recorded evidence.

\subsection{Adversarial failure modes and their defences}

The most dangerous implementation errors are not weak heuristics. They are
mistakes that quietly turn search evidence into a stronger assumption.

\begin{longtable}{@{}>{\raggedright\arraybackslash}p{0.30\textwidth}>{\raggedright\arraybackslash}p{0.64\textwidth}@{}}
\toprule

\midrule

\subsection{The proof-cost budget}

Search can produce a valid but impractically large certificate, and proof size
is a resource distinct from search time. Track certificate term count,
polynomial support size, rewrite-trace length, proof-DAG size, final proof-term
size, replay time, and axiom-audit time.

Share repeated polynomial normalisations and repeated nonnegativity proofs.
Emit a proof DAG topologically as local lemmas rather than re-expanding shared
subterms. Prefer sparse certificates and small rational coefficients when
several valid certificates are available --- but never alter a certificate
approximately in order to reduce its size, and never delete a dependency
because it looks unused in a heuristic trace while remaining inside the actual
proof term. Minimise a replay script only by rerunning and rechecking the
minimised version.

When a proof is rejected for size or replay budget, return a useful \unknown{}
diagnostic and preserve the candidate certificate as a debugging artefact. Do
not accept a partial proof, and do not silently raise the trust level in order
to finish the goal.

\subsection{The external certificate protocol}

Exchange structured data, never executable code. A problem request carries a
schema version, registered variable identifiers, exact rational coefficients,
normalised relation kinds, input bounds, and a resource envelope. A returned
certificate may reference only registered expressions and hypotheses; the Lean
side reconstructs the original semantics from its own registry.

The parser must reject malformed dimensions, duplicate or non-canonical
monomials where canonical form is required, negative exponents, oversized
integers, unknown references, invalid tree splits, and unsupported certificate
variants. It must not use \code{eval}, run a returned script, load arbitrary
shared libraries, or trust a theorem name supplied by the external solver.
Limits should be enforced \emph{before} expensive polynomial expansion as well
as after it, and search processes should be isolated from the proof-checking
process.

The packaged decoders demonstrate bounded rational parsing with numerator and
denominator bit-length caps, arity, term-count, total-degree, tree-depth and
file-size limits, and duplicate-key rejection. They are research tooling, not
hardened services for hostile uploads. A denial of service is not a soundness
failure, but it still makes a tactic unusable.
