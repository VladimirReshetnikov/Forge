\section{Provenance: what came from where}
\label{app:provenance}

This document is a merge of thirty-six independently prepared proposals for
the same system, in four rounds. The \emph{design round} \src{p1}--\src{p9}
was merged first. The \emph{extension round} \src{e1}--\src{e9} was then
prepared against that merged draft, at commit
\texttt{674521027d968d59f7b83220ed52304a85cb55e2}, and merged in turn. The
\emph{third round} \src{r1}--\src{r9} was prepared against \emph{that}
result, at commit \texttt{c98e47c5f804e92880fc1d0e378c1b95832b685c}. The
\emph{fourth round} \src{s1}--\src{s9} was prepared against the result of
\emph{that} merge, at commit
\texttt{58ea206bd7ad501b930add568151217bcc7f82a2}, and is merged here. All
thirty-six are retained verbatim under \code{proposals/} so that every claim
here can be audited against its source. Almost nothing in this article is new
mathematics: each statement is carried over from at least one of the
thirty-six, or from the two external sources named in
\S\ref{sec:external-sources}, and this appendix records which.

Neither later round is a second opinion on the one before it. Their proposals
read the merged draft, took its capabilities as prerequisites rather than
contributions, and several of them say so in their opening pages. The third
round read this document closely enough to correct its status ledger: one of
them notes that the merge ``records successful elaboration of sixteen core-only
Lean files'' and that ``it would therefore be wrong to say that nothing in
Forge has ever compiled''. Where this appendix records
an extension-round item, it is normally an addition to a section the design
round had already written, and occasionally a correction to it.

\subsection{Ideas that survive in exactly one source}

These were the merge's first concern, since a careless consolidation loses
precisely the material that distinguishes one submission from the rest.

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.4em]
\item \textbf{Square-factor and Sturm certificates} (\src{p1}) --- the only
  mechanism anywhere in the collection that can certify a polynomial with an
  interior zero, and the constructive answer to Theorem~\ref{thm:zero}.
\item \textbf{Integer-lattice elimination with a divisibility obstruction}
  (\src{p7}) --- the only worker that emits a certificate of \emph{infeasibility}.
\item \textbf{Finite-residue witnesses} (\src{p4}) --- the only
  existential-over-$\Z$-by-case-analysis certificate.
\item \textbf{Polynomial witnesses discharged by two cone certificates}
  (\src{p9}) --- the smallest complete instance of the composition pattern the
  whole architecture argues for.
\item \textbf{Farkas affine witnesses as one feasibility problem} (\src{p2}) ---
  the observation that synthesis and proof search are jointly linear.
\item \textbf{Proof-logging CDCL(T) with independent RUP re-derivation}
  (\src{p6}), and the resolution-DAG alternative (\src{p4}).
\item \textbf{Theory-constrained quantifier instantiation} (\src{p2}) ---
  emitting an equality constraint at an interpreted subterm instead of failing.
\item \textbf{Certificate cost inside the scheduling benefit} (\src{p8}).
\item \textbf{The quantitative interior-zero coefficient}
  $(\ell-1/3)(u-1/3)<0$ (\src{p3}).
\item \textbf{The rank-one Gram argument} for $(x+y+1)^2$ (\src{p9}) and the
  \textbf{weight-6-versus-coefficient-4 argument} for $(2x-3y)^2$ (\src{p7}).
\item \textbf{The Lean design contracts} --- the runtime and scheduler contract
  (\src{p3}) and the \code{CertificateSpec} soundness contract (\src{p6}); these
  complement rather than duplicate each other, and both now elaborate.
\end{itemize}

From the extension round:

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.4em]
\item \textbf{Finite Kripke countermodels for \textsf{IPC}} (\src{e4}) --- the
  only mechanism in the collection that turns a failed intuitionistic search
  into an object a second program can re-check, and the direct answer to the
  gap \S\ref{sec:external-sources} identifies below.
\item \textbf{Cyclic proof compilation through checked descent} (\src{e7}) ---
  the only treatment of recursive obligations that neither trusts a cyclic graph
  nor abandons the pattern; with the tagged-state rank, the phase measure, and
  size-change closure demoted to a filter.
\item \textbf{Continuation-local synthesis} (\src{e7}), with the algebra
  region and the natural join over shared hole identifiers.
\item \textbf{Exact one-output integer projection} (\src{e8}) --- the only
  worker that replaces a modulus-sized table with a hash-consed straight-line
  program, and the only one that discloses a \emph{shared} search/check
  assembler rather than claiming independence.
\item \textbf{Finite-algebra covers with concrete counterexample trees}
  (\src{e8}) --- the cheapest checker anywhere here: a bare list of state
  identifiers.
\item \textbf{Ore common-left-multiple certificates with a complete natural-root
  cover} (\src{e9}) --- the only treatment of recurrence transport that makes
  the checker find the singularities itself, via an explicit Cauchy bound.
\item \textbf{The $D-1$ separating-word bound with attaining fixtures}
  (\src{e5}, \src{e9}) --- delayed-error families that are correct on every
  input shorter than the bound.
\item \textbf{The interpolation-grid counterexample} (\src{e9}) ---
  $x'=x+\prod_{i=0}^{6}(u-i)$, zero on every small input set and nonzero at
  $u=7$; the regression control against mistaking a grid for a sample.
\item \textbf{The retained failed-run history} (\src{e9}) --- three recorded
  failures with their tracebacks, including one whose diagnosis the package
  explicitly declines to claim it proved.
\item \textbf{The span/ideal two-lane ablation} (\src{e1}) --- the same
  problems through two certificate languages, sixteen unknown against sixteen
  proved.
\end{itemize}

From the third round:

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.4em]
\item \textbf{The expectation/homomorphism correction} (\src{r7}) --- the only
  claim in twenty-seven proposals that a rule already in this document would
  prove something false if extended in the obvious way, together with a type
  distinction that makes the unsound step unwritable.
\item \textbf{Degree-complete noncommutative refutation} (\src{r2}) --- a
  third species of checkable negative object, refuting an algebra identity by
  exhibiting an algebra.
\item \textbf{The observable quotient} (\src{r1}) --- the only construction
  anywhere here that changes the state space rather than describing it.
\item \textbf{Ghost rates} (\src{r4}) and \textbf{the minimality receipt}
  (\src{r6}) --- two different ways to be better than a plain ladder, one
  widening the accepted language and one shortening the certificate.
\item \textbf{Anchored remainders that preserve the order of vanishing}
  (\src{r5}) --- the property that makes them composable with a jet certificate
  instead of destroying the information it needs.
\item \textbf{Pop summaries with proof sharing} (\src{r9}) --- a finite
  certificate for an unbounded stack, and the only certificate format in the
  collection with shared subproofs.
\item \textbf{The properness potential} (\src{r9}) --- one witness yielding
  three exact values, and the ingredient without which a closure identity does
  not reach an infinite horizon.
\item \textbf{Threshold-local and resetting ranks} (\src{r8}, \src{r9}) ---
  the repair for a descent criterion that rejects correct recurrent behaviour.
\item \textbf{Shipping no Lean, deliberately} (\src{r9}) --- the only
  proposal to refuse the practice the other twenty-six follow, and the only one
  vindicated by a compiler.
\end{itemize}

From the fourth round:

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.4em]
\item \textbf{The two-sided frontier} (\src{s1}) --- the only coverability
  certificate that proves exactness and minimality rather than safety, and the
  only one carrying a positive run per basis element.
\item \textbf{The direct-cover receipt} (\src{s4}) --- the only certificate
  format anywhere here that avoids materialising an object it reasons about,
  turning $26{,}075{,}972{,}546$ minimal predecessors into a one-leaf tree.
\item \textbf{Bell-number finiteness} (\src{s3}) --- a finite certificate for
  an infinite alphabet that owes nothing to a well-quasi-order, and the
  discipline that an integer used as an atom name is not an integer the
  modelled program may compare.
\item \textbf{Root-index witnesses} (\src{s5}) --- an existential answered by
  a position in an ordered root list, which is checkable where a closed form
  does not exist.
\item \textbf{Zero-tail saturation} (\src{s7}) --- the repair for a projection
  that silently loses witnesses needing more digits than any visible input.
\item \textbf{Certified non-membership in a group} (\src{s8}) --- finite
  because a chain's completeness is a finite check, not because a search was
  bounded.
\item \textbf{The Reynolds reduction} (\src{s8}) --- the only worker here that

\subsection{Material from outside the nine}

Two neighbouring projects were reviewed after the merge, and a substantial
amount of this document comes from them rather than from any proposal. They
matter because between them they had \emph{implemented} the thing all nine
proposals specified and none built: a boundary at which nothing becomes a fact
without a checked Lean proof.

\item \textbf{Djex} --- a type-directed Haskell expression synthesizer merging

Two qualifications on that material.

The \code{debug.skipKernelTC} and asynchronous-\code{addDecl} findings were
\emph{verified first-hand} against the installed \code{v4.34.0} source rather
than taken on the reviewing project's word, because its own analysis is written
against \code{v4.33.0}. Both hold at the pinned version.

And one claim was corrected in the other direction. An \textsf{LJT} decision
procedure decides non-inhabitation, but the reference implementation emits for
a negative answer a single enumeration tag and no artefact --- no closed-branch
tree, nothing a second program can re-check. It is therefore \emph{weaker} than
lattice elimination, not equivalent to it, and \S\ref{sec:negative} says so.
The opportunity that follows --- reifying a finite failed search into a
checkable refutation object --- was described here as unbuilt anywhere. The
extension round built it. \src{e4}'s finite Kripke countermodels are exactly
that object: a rooted finite partial order with a persistent valuation, which a
checker re-evaluates from scratch without trusting any solver-supplied table of
formula truth values, and whose soundness theorem
(Theorem~\ref{thm:kripke}) rules out a derivation. The gap identified in this
appendix is therefore closed by a later proposal rather than by the merge, and
the \textsf{LJT} correction above stands unchanged: the countermodel is the
artefact, and the enumeration tag never was one.

The extension round read the same two neighbouring projects, at the same pinned
revisions, and adds two facts worth recording. Leant's own Djex dependency is
revision \texttt{e237e866}, which is \emph{not} the standalone Djex head
\texttt{e8778f4ebd63}; substituting one for the other when reproducing Leant's
reports would not reproduce them. And no extension proposal modified either
repository or ran a live synthesis against them; their integration sections are
designs, and \src{e8}'s says so in its own status box.

\subsection{What the merge changed}

Four things in this document are not simply inherited.

First, the twenty-seven experiment sections are reported as twenty-seven, with
the non-pooling rule stated explicitly and the four Horn setups tabulated
against each other. Several of the source articles warned against pooling their
\emph{own} numbers, and both later rounds warned against pooling with earlier
totals as well; none could warn against pooling across articles within its own
round, because none knew about its siblings. The third round made that gap
concrete: two of its packages ship a second summary file whose headline numbers
match the first exactly while describing a different run.

Second, the bibliography is deduplicated: roughly 215 \code{bibitem} entries
under about 100 keys became 56 entries under descriptive keys, keeping the
richest description of each work and preserving details only one source had
recorded --- inspected blob SHA-1s, the release timestamp, the
blob-id-is-not-a-commit-id caveat, and the \code{sos} toolchain version
mismatch.

Third, the build is plain pdf\LaTeX{} with stock packages. The nine used four
engines and nine font families between them, and every one of their READMEs had
to explain what to do when a font was missing.

Fourth, and the only new evidence: eighteen Lean files were compiled
(Tables~\ref{tab:lean}, \ref{tab:lean-extensions}, \ref{tab:lean-closure}
and~\ref{tab:lean-round-three}), and all eighteen later suites were re-run and
reproduced their recorded counts (Tables~\ref{tab:extension-rerun}
and~\ref{tab:round-three-runs}). Every one of the twenty-seven sources recorded
\code{NOT\_RUN} for Lean. That is now narrower --- six of the twenty-one files
in the merged tree, ten of the twenty extension-round source files, and two of
the third round's three core-only files elaborate under Lean v4.34.0, eleven of
the eighteen with no axiom dependencies at all --- and the limits of what it
shows are stated where the result appears. Three of those
six are the merge's own: the ten extension specimens contained six spellings of
one reachability theorem and five of one word-fold theorem, so deduplicating
them was itself a merge decision rather than a transcription.

Fifth, and specific to the later rounds: those proposals were prepared
independently and overlap heavily, so the merge had to decide in each case
whether two similar-looking results are the same result. Several are not. Three
different greatest-fixed-point theorems, three different certificate namespaces,
two identically named PDFs, and three different strengths of producer/checker
separation are all recorded above as distinctions rather than flattened into
agreement.

The third round needed the same judgement three more times, and in two of them
the answer went the other way. Its two noncommutative soundness theorems
\emph{are} the same theorem with the same proof, and its three
integrating-factor lemmas are the same lemma --- two stated identically, the
third generalised to an arbitrary anchor. Those are stated once each, in
\S\ref{sec:noncommutative} and \S\ref{sec:analytic}. What is \emph{not}
the same, despite appearances, is \src{r1}'s closure and the closure already
in \S\ref{sec:closure}: one closes functionals under pullback, the other
values under pushforward, and they are dual rather than identical. Getting that
backwards would have merged away the only quotient construction in the
document.

Sixth, and new to this round: the merge compiled delivered Lean and found it
broken. That had not been possible before, because every proposal in the first
two rounds recorded \code{NOT\_RUN}. The failure, its one-line cause, and the
tested repair are recorded together in
\texttt{results/lean-round-three-elaboration.json}, and the archived source is
left exactly as delivered.

\subsection{Conventions used in this document}

\code{\textbackslash status\{...\}} banners, inherited from several of the
sources, mark whether a mechanism was implemented and executed, specified only,
or proposed for production. \src{pN}, \src{eN}, \src{rN} and \src{sN} labels
attribute a result to the design-round, extension-round, third-round or
fourth-round run that produced it. \unknown{} always means a bounded search found nothing; it never
means a statement is false.
