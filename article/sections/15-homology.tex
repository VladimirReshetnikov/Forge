\section{Chain complexes: coordinates, torsion, and an obstruction that homology cannot see}
\label{sec:homology}

The last contributing effort of the fourth round opens the collection's only
homological algebra. Its objects are finitely generated free $\Z$-modules with
integer matrices $A,B$ satisfying $BA=0$, and its question is the cycle-modulo-
boundary one: given a cycle, is it a boundary, and if not, what exactly is in
the way?

Section~\ref{sec:structural} already solves integer systems, by row-by-row
unimodular elimination with a divisibility obstruction. This effort is explicit
that it does not claim a new integer solver --- it lists ``Smith normal form as
a new algorithm'' under what it is \emph{not} claiming, and reuses a lattice
solver as its inner engine. What is new is what the receipts have to carry so
that several such solutions stay compatible with each other.

\subsection{Coordinates that survive being used together}

A single Smith decomposition answers one question. This worker needs the cycle
space, a set of representatives, the coordinate map, the relation matrix and
the induced map to agree simultaneously, so its certificate carries a matched
family: a kernel basis, a coordinate map $X$ and representatives $R$ with
$XR=I$ and $BR=0$, the invariant factors, and --- for each concrete question ---
either a filler $w$ with $Aw$ equal to the given cycle, or an obstruction.

The obstruction is a row functional $\phi$ together with a modulus $q$: $\phi$
vanishes on every boundary but not on the given cycle modulo $q$. That is the
same species of witness the existing lattice worker emits, and the effort says
so. What the existing worker does not produce is the rest of the family, and
the certificate exists so that a filler found for one cycle and a coordinate
map used for another are answers about the same decomposition.

\begin{remark}[Cycle-restricted exactness is not ambient splitting]
The effort's sharpest scope condition, and the one a naive integration would
get wrong. With $B=(2)$, the middle-exactness statement ``every cycle is a
boundary'' can hold while the stronger ambient identity --- that the sequence
splits --- fails. A checker that encoded exactness as a splitting identity
would reject correct inputs, and a source bridge that assumed it would prove
something else. The theorem is about $\ker B/\operatorname{im}A$ and nothing
wider.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Reductions, and why the middle term matters}

A chain reduction is a triple $(f,g,h)$ between a complex and a smaller one,
with $h$ a chain homotopy witnessing that the composites are the identity up to
boundary. Cancelling a unit entry in a differential produces one; composing
reductions produces another. The point is that a solution found in the reduced
complex lifts back, and the lifting formula is
\[
 H=h_D T+g_D\bar H f_C+g_D f_D T h_C,
\]
in which all three terms are needed. The effort keeps a fixture whose reduced
homotopy is nonzero precisely so that the middle term is exercised, rather than
testing only complexes that reduce to zero and in which it vanishes silently.

The recorded ablation is a dimension experiment: a family in which the direct
system grows to 288 equations in 144 variables, exceeding the search cap, while
the reduced system has \emph{zero} equations and the lifted witness is checked
against the original problem. The effort's own caption is the right reading of
it --- ``a capability observation under a stated cap, not a general speedup'',
on a family chosen to contain cancellable summands, with no claim about how
often such summands occur.

\subsection{The obstruction homology cannot see}

The result that earns this section its place is a negative one, and it is a
kind of negative the collection did not previously have.

Two chain maps can induce \emph{equal maps on all homology groups} and still
fail to be chain homotopic. The certificate for that failure is not a
divisibility obstruction on a lattice system; it is a matrix $W$ satisfying an
adjoint condition under the Frobenius pairing, which separates the difference
$F-G$ from the image of the homotopy operator. The smallest instance is the
equation $2a+2b=1$: unsolvable over $\Z$, and the reason two maps agreeing
everywhere homology can see are nonetheless different.

\begin{remark}[Three species of negative evidence, now four]
Section~\ref{sec:structural} refutes derivability by a Kripke countermodel.
Section~\ref{sec:closure} refutes an automaton equivalence by a separating
word. Section~\ref{sec:noncommutative} refutes an algebra identity by
exhibiting an algebra. This refutes a homotopy by exhibiting a functional, and
it differs from all three in what it tells you: not that the statement is
false, but that \emph{the invariant you computed is too coarse to decide it}.
An obstruction of that kind is a reason to compute something finer, which is a
different instruction to a planner than a counterexample is.
\end{remark}

The checker for the negative runs a structurally different computation from the
producer that found it --- a pairing evaluation against a linear solve --- which
is a stronger producer/checker separation than most of this document's lanes
achieve.

\subsection{What it is not, and one convention that would break it}

An algebraic statement about a supplied differential is not a topological one.
Acyclicity, chain-homotopy equivalence, simple-homotopy equivalence and
topological homotopy equivalence are four different claims, and matching
homology summaries establish none of the others. The effort refuses to infer a
topological theorem from a homology computation, and says so.

For the Lean statement, two details are recorded and both matter. A
\code{Homotopy} in Mathlib is \emph{data}, not a proposition, so the negative
result is stated as $\Not(\Nonempty(\ldots))$ rather than by negating a
structure. And Mathlib's \code{comm} field is arranged as $F=dH+Hd+G$ where
this worker's matrices satisfy $F-G=dH+Hd$; the adapter must prove that
rearrangement rather than paste a matrix into a field with a different
convention. Mathlib supplies the homotopy object and the theorem that homotopic
maps induce equal homology maps. What it does not supply is the constructive
search, the reflected checker, or the source binding --- which is the work.
