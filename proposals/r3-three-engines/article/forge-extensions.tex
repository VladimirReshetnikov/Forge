\begin{abstract}
Forge already has a substantial design for certificate-producing proof planning above Lean's \grind{}: induction and invariant synthesis, arithmetic witnesses, polynomial certificates, finite closures, and several external proof-search interfaces. Recommending these again would not significantly extend it. This study instead develops three comparatively independent additions.

The first turns exact polyhedral projection into a constructive procedure for universally quantified parameters and multiple existential real outputs. It accepts mixed strict and non-strict affine constraints and extracts a shared min--max expression rather than searching a fixed affine template. A four-corner example proves a strict expressiveness separation from affine witnesses. The second operates in a free associative algebra: a bounded, traced completion procedure discovers missing noncommutative lemmas, while a much smaller checker verifies only a two-sided ideal identity in the original relations. The third supplies rational enclosures for elementary functions and combines them with exact vanishing-jet certificates, so a non-strict inequality with an interior zero need not be defeated by numerical uncertainty at that zero.

All three workers were prototyped and exercised. Their separate workloads contain 110 polyhedral cases, 85 noncommutative cases, and 44 analytic cases. Expected negative and resource-limited outcomes are retained rather than counted as failures or silently discarded. Replay is performed with search imports blocked and site packages disabled. Additional evidence comprises an exact vertex-enumeration oracle, polynomial-action checks, witness evaluations, mutation rejection, and optional numerical sanity checks. None of this is reported as a Lean-kernel proof. The final sections specify the source reification obligations, checker theorems, library reuse, and release gates required to turn these prototypes into actual Forge capabilities.
\end{abstract}
