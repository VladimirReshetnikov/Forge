\section{Integration into Forge and Lean}
\label{sec:integration}

\subsection{Extend the workers, not the architecture}

Forge already separates problem representation, proposed evidence, checking,
and publication into a source proof. Its \code{CertificateSpec} contract
contains a denotation, a Boolean check, and a theorem that accepted certificates
imply that denotation~\cite{forge-contract}. None of the new algorithms requires
a replacement scheduler or a second general-purpose proof search framework.
They should become workers under that existing interface.

\subsection{A small neutral problem language}

Use four first-order input forms matching the delivered dataclasses. Each has
an explicit semantic version, supplied by the eventual enclosing protocol.
The Python research format discriminates by \code{kind}; it does not yet
implement Forge's full versioned worker transport.

\begin{longtable}{@{}L{0.19\linewidth}L{0.42\linewidth}L{0.32\linewidth}@{}}
\toprule
Problem & Required source data & Semantic choices to preserve \\
\midrule
\endhead
Parity arena & Owners, priorities, total legal-successor relation, queried
initial state or full-region query & Max recurrent priority; strategy
observations; legal adversary moves \\
Transport & Two normalized rational distributions and a relation matrix & Full
marginals; defect event; equality or budget requested \\
Simulation & Source and target action distributions; observation relation &
$\forall a\exists b$ before sampling; actions as source-owned identities \\
Expected time & Actions, absorbing terminals, initial state & Unit cost before
termination; supremum over all schedulers \\
\bottomrule
\end{longtable}

Do not erase a terminal predicate, an action owner, or a probabilistic choice
into an uninterpreted label and then infer its semantics from the solver's
answer. In particular, a parameterized probability can be reified as a rational
only after the source term has been proved equal to that rational. Substituting
a decimal approximation is a different theorem.

The source language can begin with finite enumerated states and commands
\code{pure}, finite rational \code{sample}, \code{bind}, and an explicit
finite action choice. Reification produces a transition table \emph{and} a
proof of its meaning, by compositional equations for these commands. This is
much smaller than translating arbitrary Lean programs or arbitrary recursive
functions to a probabilistic model.

\subsection{Source bridges as the critical new obligations}

\paragraph{Enumeration.}
For a finite source state type $\alpha$, use a proved equivalence
$e:\alpha\simeq\mathrm{Fin}\,n$, or a supplied finite cover with the appropriate
semantic theorem. A list of distinct sample states is not a proof that the
state space has been covered. For dependent actions, enumeration is per source
state, not a single untyped action number reused across unrelated states.

\paragraph{Transition interpretation.}
For a probabilistic command $K:\alpha\to\mathrm{PMF}\,\alpha$, the bridge must
establish the equality between its point masses and the rational table lifted
to nonnegative extended reals. Nonnegativity and total mass one are proved
before the lift. At the pinned Mathlib revision,
\code{Mathlib.Probability.ProbabilityMassFunction.Monad} defines
\code{PMF.pure}, \code{PMF.bind}, and \code{PMF.bind_apply}; these are actual
inspected declarations, not speculative API names~\cite{mathlib-pmf}.
The corresponding \code{Basic}, \code{Constructions}, and \code{Integrals}
modules are present in the same directory. The first bridge should stay on
finite types so that its essential algebra reduces to finite sums.

\paragraph{Strategies.}
Reconstruct a policy as a dependent function selecting a legal action, not as a
list of integers whose legality is assumed. A winning-region theorem should
state its universal environment quantifier explicitly. Any available-information
restriction on the strategy is part of the source type. The prototype has
perfect state information; it is not a solver for partially observable games.

\paragraph{Path and stopping semantics.}
The first expected-time theorem can use finite stopped execution trees.
Define $H_0(h)=0$ for each history $h$. If its last state is terminal, define
$H_{N+1}(h)=0$; otherwise take one plus the finite weighted average of
$H_N$ over the next action and state. Induction gives
$H_N(h)\le V(\operatorname{last}(h))$ under the Bellman inequalities, even when
the action weights depend on all of $h$. The theorem equating the increasing
limit of these quantities with $\E[T]$ can then use the established probability
library. A result about $H_{100}$ alone is not an unbounded termination proof.

\subsection{Proposed module boundaries}

The following names are a proposed addition, not files already present in
Forge:
\begin{lstlisting}
Forge/Finite/Distribution.lean
  normalized finite rational tables; interpretation and exact sums
Forge/Relational/Transport.lean
  coupling checker; marginal soundness; Hall lower bound; bind rule
Forge/Game/Parity.lean
  strategy closure; threshold-rank checker; infinite-play theorem
Forge/Probabilistic/Simulation.lean
  supported matches; deletion-trace soundness; greatestness theorem
Forge/Probabilistic/Bellman.lean
  finite-horizon bound; exact tight-policy result; trap refutation
Forge/Reify/FinitePrograms.lean
  source enumerations, command semantics, action identities
\end{lstlisting}

Keep the mathematical theorems separate from reflection. For example, prove
that \emph{any} matrix with the specified marginals is a coupling, then prove
that the Boolean matrix checker establishes those equations. Likewise, prove
Theorem~\ref{thm:bellman-upper} about a semantic transition kernel once, rather
than generating a new optional-stopping proof for each certificate.

The emitted source proof should consist of a checked certificate application
followed by the source bridge. It must not rerun search during ordinary
recompilation. Large data can be represented as shared arrays or a compact
certificate declaration, but a compact representation still needs a theorem
connecting its decoded entries to the denotation.

\subsection{Result types should name their authority}

Each lane needs more than a Boolean success flag. The following are suggested
semantic outcomes, translated into Forge's existing publication mechanism:
\begin{lstlisting}
Parity:
  WinningStrategy(player, region, checked witness)
Transport:
  OptimalDefect(value, coupling, Hall witness)
Simulation:
  GreatestSimulation(relation, matches, exclusion trace)
Expected time:
  ExactWorstExpectation(value, tight policy, Bellman witness)
  PositiveNonterminationProbability(prefix, trap, lower bound)
Common:
  Unsupported(fragment reason) | Unknown(resource reason)
\end{lstlisting}

A transport optimum above zero refutes perfect $R$-couplability, not equality in
distribution for every relation. A simulation exclusion refutes membership in
that specified simulation. An exact worst expectation above a budget supplies
a policy violating the budget even when all policies still terminate. An
MDP trap proves nontermination with positive probability, not merely a large
expected duration.

For a source proposition $G$, only the existing certificate-soundness theorem
and a proved bridge may construct a \code{CheckedOutcome.closed} proof.
Model-level negative evidence with no reverse bridge is a diagnostic or a new
model theorem, not a proof of $\neg G$. This reuses Forge's distinction between
authority and search guidance without repeating its general security design.

\subsection{How to use \grind{} without treating it as a model checker}

The surrounding proof can still use \grind{} for equalities, bounds on indices,
case normalization, and local logical consequences~\cite{grind}. For instance,
a source invariant may reduce a parameterized transition probability to one of
a few rational values. A certificate worker should be requested after that
normalization has produced a proved finite problem, not before.

Conversely, the worker can return a proved quantitative fact to the local
solver: an optimal defect is zero, a bound is at most the user's budget, or an
initial state belongs to a winning region. This is genuine theory cooperation.
It does not require exposing the whole max-flow residual network or the entire
policy iteration history to \grind{}.
