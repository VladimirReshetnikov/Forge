\section{Implementation and experimental evidence}
\label{sec:experiments}

\subsection{Execution conditions and units}

The full run used Python 3.13.5 on
\code{Linux-6.18.44-x86_64-with-glibc2.41}, with seed \code{20260915}. It was
invoked with \code{python -S}, which disables site packages. Every generated
problem and its accepted certificate is retained in
\path{results/full/corpus.json}. The run conditions and counts appear in
\path{results/full/report.json}. A smaller smoke run is retained separately
and is not added to the full-run totals.

Fourteen focused regression tests also passed with no failures or errors.
Mutation counts differ because a second semantic mutation is generated only

\subsection{Parity: exhaustive small arenas and random larger ones}

The exhaustive part includes every total arena on one or two vertices, with
both owner choices and priorities from $\{0,1,2,3\}$. There are 8 one-vertex
arenas and 576 two-vertex arenas, hence 584 in total. A further 300 seeded
arenas have 3--8 vertices, one or two successors per vertex, random owners,
and priorities from $\{0,\ldots,5\}$.

The oracle does not call Zielonka or check threshold ranks. It enumerates
positional strategies of both players and follows the resulting deterministic
run to its eventual cycle. A player wins a start vertex when it has a strategy
winning against every positional strategy of the opponent. Finite positional
determinacy makes this an independent small-arena decision computation.
Every returned winning partition agreed with this oracle.

The mutations remove a vertex from the required partition, or deliberately
violate one applicable strict threshold inequality. The semantic regressions
also test a one-time favorable priority, an infinite favorable cycle, and
an omitted adversarial edge. These controls target the certificate theorem,
not merely the solver's ability to reproduce its own partition.

\subsection{Transport: exact optima and an actual ablation}

The 400 instances use marginal dimensions independently chosen from 1--6.
Unnormalized integer weights are chosen from 0--4, with an all-zero vector
repaired by assigning positive weight to one coordinate, then normalized
exactly. Relation entries are seeded Boolean choices.

The oracle enumerates every subset $A$ of the left support and computes
$\max_A h(A)$ directly. It never constructs a residual network. Each
max-flow proposal's exact defect agreed with this maximum.
Of the 400 optima, 77 were zero and 323 were positive. Both are valid completed
answers because the certificate proves the optimum, not merely feasibility.

For the same input, the experiment also evaluates the defect of the independent
product $\mu\otimes\nu$. On \textbf{211 of 400 instances}, the synthesized
coupling has strictly smaller defect. On the others it ties; it is never
worse. This is a genuine paired ablation of a weaker relational method, not a
comparison with another Lean tactic. It establishes that optimizing correlation
matters in these generated cases; it does not establish a population success
rate for source-level verification problems.

One mutation corrupts a joint entry, violating a marginal. When the optimum is
positive, another erases the Hall subset, creating a nonzero primal--dual gap.
All are rejected. Separate regressions reject missing probability mass,
noncanonical rational numbers, and independent-product data masquerading as a
zero-defect correlation certificate.

\subsection{Expected time: a structurally different oracle}

The 180 MDPs have 2--4 states, one or two actions per nonterminal state, a single
absorbing terminal state, and initial state 0. Marginals use the same exact
integer-weight generator. In every even-indexed case, each nonterminal action
is modified to put at least probability $1/2$ directly on the terminal state;
these are controlled positive fixtures embedded in the random workload.
Odd-indexed cases have no such guarantee.

The oracle enumerates stationary deterministic policies. For each policy it
uses graph reachability to detect a reachable closed nonterminal class. When
none exists, it computes the finite expected duration by determinant expansion
and Cramer's rule on the reachable nonterminal subsystem. This deliberately
avoids the proposer's Gauss--Jordan routine. It then takes the worst policy
value, or reports nontermination when an appropriate policy exists.

The proposer returned \textbf{164 exact finite worst-expectation certificates}.
The remaining \textbf{16 cases} returned positive-probability nontermination
certificates. Every
outcome and every finite initial-state value agreed with the oracle. The
negative certificate's positive path probability was separately rechecked.
Mutating a positive result's initial potential to zero or a negative result's
probability lower bound to zero was rejected in every case.

The tiny sizes are intentional: exhaustive policy enumeration and determinant
expansion provide a transparent differential check, not a competitive
large-MDP solver. A follow-up performance evaluation should retain these
oracles for small cases while separately studying larger models; it should not
replace exact small-case validation with agreement between two wrappers around
the same numerical solver.

\subsection{Simulation: enumerate relations, not elimination traces}

The 100 pairs of systems have 1--2 states on the left and 1--3 on the right,
one or two actions per state, and a seeded Boolean base relation. The oracle
enumerates every subrelation of that base and checks its post-fixed property
using exhaustive Hall-subset tests. The union of all post-fixed relations is
then the greatest simulation. This is a different computation from the
proposer's ordered pair-removal loop.

The resulting greatest relations agreed on every problem. Across this workload,
77 pair deletions and 81 surviving pairs were recorded; these are
\emph{pair counts}, not problem counts or solved theorems. Invalid tests erase
a strict Hall witness or remove a required action match when such data exists.
The quantifier-order regression explicitly requires different target actions
for different source actions.

\subsection{Replay without search or test oracles}

The command
\begin{lstlisting}
cd prototype
python -S replay.py ../results/full/corpus.json \
  --receipt ../results/full/replay.json
\end{lstlisting}
was executed. The replay process installs an import hook that rejects imports
of \code{forge_ap.search} and the entire \code{tests} namespace, then imports
only representations and the checker. It accepted all stored records:
884 parity, 400 transport, 180 MDP, and 100 simulation records. The receipt
records \code{search_imports = FORBIDDEN} and
\code{site_packages_enabled = false}.

This is an operational separation test, not a formal proof of software
independence. The search, checker, and oracle still share input dataclasses,
normalization rules, Python integers, and rational arithmetic. A defect in a
shared representation or an incorrect mathematical model could affect every
computation. The independence claim is specifically that the checking
algorithm does not invoke the search algorithms or the finite test oracles.

\subsection{What the measured timings do and do not mean}

The recorded full test run took approximately 0.414 seconds and the replay
approximately 0.070 seconds in this environment. These figures are retained
as raw execution metadata, not as performance evidence. They combine tiny
synthetic cases, local arithmetic, serialization, and test bookkeeping; they
are not repeated measurements and have no Lean baseline. No speedup or
throughput claim should be derived from their ratio.

The algorithm counters are similarly diagnostic: the parity proposer made
2,884 recursive calls, the transport workload used 1,219 augmentations, the
MDP workload used 383 trap-iteration passes and 231 policy evaluations, and
the simulation workload invoked transport search 386 times. These counters
have different units and are deliberately not combined into a scalar
``proof effort'' score.

\subsection{Remaining implementation weaknesses}

The research parser rejects duplicate keys, floating-point and nonfinite JSON
numbers, noncanonical rational pairs, out-of-range indices, and malformed
array shapes. Its bounds are not a hostile-input security design. In
particular, it bounds individual arrays but does not enforce one global budget
for all matrix cells or intermediate rational arithmetic. Resource-limit
rejections must be distinguished from malformed input at a production adapter;
neither implies that a mathematical proposition is false.

The code is small and intentionally dense in places. Its searches are not
interruptible Forge workers; they do not yet receive cancellation tokens,
subprocess resource quotas, or snapshot handles. The Python checker is not
formally verified, and the Lean specimens do not implement a decoder or source
reifier. These are concrete remaining tasks, not hidden assumptions behind the
passing tests.
