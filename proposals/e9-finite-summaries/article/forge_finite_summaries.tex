\vspace*{12mm}
{\color{muted}\Large A CONCRETE EXTENSION TO THE FORGE DESIGN\par}
\vspace{12mm}
{\color{ink}\Huge\bfseries Certified Finite Summaries\\[4pt] for Infinite Proofs\par}
\vspace{8mm}
{\LARGE Weighted words, boundary-safe telescoping,\\[3pt] and singular recurrence transport\par}
\vspace{13mm}
{\large Prepared for Vladimir Reshetnikov\par}
{\large 15 September 2026\par}
\vspace{12mm}
\begin{abstract}
Forge already proposes a broad certificate-producing planner around Lean's
\grind{}. This article adds three concrete mathematical capabilities rather than
another planner architecture: exact equivalence checking for finite-dimensional
word computations, creative telescoping with explicitly safe support boundaries,
and recurrence proof composition that accounts for singular leading
coefficients. A finite matrix simulation can prove an identity for every list,
including lists over an infinite polynomial input alphabet. A shifted-binomial
flux can prove a recurrence for a definite hypergeometric sum without treating
an undefined product at a boundary as zero. Checked left multiples of Ore
operators then let independently obtained recurrences meet, while a finite
singularity cover determines which additional values must be proved equal.

The accompanying Python implementation produces 245 checkable entries in one
recorded corpus: 54 matrix closure certificates, 115 separating words, 23
summation certificates, six Ore identities, and 47 singularity plans. All replay
with Python site packages disabled. The corpus includes 100 differential
word-model tests, 245 rejected invalidating mutations, and two honestly recorded
insufficient-ansatz outcomes; 41 additional unit tests pass. These are exact
Python checks, not Lean proofs. The article gives soundness arguments, precise
fragment boundaries, a library map, proposed Lean modules, and implementation
acceptance gates. No integrated tactic, kernel-checked certificate, or performance
comparison against an existing Lean tactic is claimed.
\end{abstract}
