\begin{abstract}
Forge already has a broad design for proof planning above Lean's \code{grind}.
This article develops additional algorithms rather than another description of
that architecture. The central addition computes the greatest finite-dimensional
space of invariant generators whose transition pullbacks admit degree-bounded
polynomial multipliers. A descending linear-algebra calculation replaces the
apparently bilinear simultaneous search for generators and multipliers. Two
further workers prove properties of all finite recursively constructed trees by
finite-algebra covers, and eliminate one existential integer variable while
constructing a symbolic witness without modulus-sized residue tables. A fourth,
unimplemented integration proposal decomposes higher-order synthesis into
proof-directed fold contracts, motivated by a specific open composition problem
in Leant and Djex.

The archive contains exact standard-library Python implementations, checks of
\ConstCertificates{} constant-multiplier packets, \IdealCertificates{}
polynomial-multiplier packets, \FiniteCertificates{} finite-algebra certificates,
and \IntegerCertificates{} symbolic integer projections. It also contains
\UnitTestCount{} passing test methods, a differential integer experiment with
\IntegerValuations{} concrete parameter valuations, and explicit incompleteness
and corruption controls. These are Python-checked research artifacts, not Lean
kernel proofs or a measured improvement over an existing tactic. The mathematical
soundness and fragment-completeness claims are proved in the text; the remaining
Lean implementation work is specified separately.
\end{abstract}

\section{The delta: what this article adds}
\label{sec:delta}

\subsection{The starting point is already ambitious}
At the audited revision, Forge's merged article already specifies an outer
proof-planning layer, demand-directed theorem use, induction and accumulator
generalization, lemma discovery, arithmetic witness synthesis, exact algebraic
certificates, and a carefully scoped verification boundary. Its structural
chapter implements polynomial recurrence discovery and conserved quantities;
it explicitly identifies more general simultaneous invariant preservation as a
next step. The repository also implements finite residue tables for integer
witnesses. Those are the immediate baselines for this article, not capabilities
being proposed again~\cite{forge-readme,forge-structural,forge-conclusion}.

The Leant/Djex review already incorporated into Forge is substantial. It covers
source-owned evidence, negative-verdict restrictions, kernel-checking options,
process boundaries, neutral identities, and budget accounting. Repeating that
material would not make the tactic more powerful~\cite{forge-neighbors}.
Here the additions are concrete, separately checkable inference mechanisms.

\midrule

The first three rows have executable prototypes in the archive. The fourth is a
detailed integration design and two hand-written Lean specimens; it is not a
claim that the original Leant query has been solved. Repository searches for
\code{pullback}, \code{automata}, and \code{Cooper} returned no matches, but that
is not an exhaustive proof of absence from every frozen proposal. The novelty
claim is a concrete extension of the reviewed merged design and implementation,
not invention of tree automata, Presburger arithmetic, or abstract interpretation.

\subsection{A common problem, not another common framework}
The new workers address three different reasons local proof search gets stuck.
A true equality may need to be proved \emph{together with} other equalities that
change under the transition. A recursive object may be unbounded while its
relevant semantic summary is finite. An existential integer may require a
piecewise, periodic function of parameters rather than a rational affine term.
The higher-order proposal addresses a fourth issue: a correct component can
exist in the search space yet be buried by irrelevant same-typed alternatives.

Each worker changes the mathematical object searched for. It does not merely
increase a timeout or select a larger theorem collection. Successful output is,
respectively, an invariant packet, an inductive cover or concrete tree, and a
witness program with a guard. Their source-level meanings are distinct and
must remain distinct in Forge's result types.

\subsection{Evidence and scope}
All experiments in this archive ran in one environment and are reproducible
with Python's standard library. The checkers use exact integers and rational
numbers. They do not use numerical tolerances. Nevertheless, shared arithmetic
code and shared certificate elaboration remain possible common-mode failure
sources. The archive has no Lean executable available, no implementation of the
\code{forge} tactic, and no benchmark against \code{grind}, Aesop, or another
Lean automation tool. The optional Lean files are explicitly marked
\textsc{not run}. These distinctions are essential when interpreting the tables
in Section~\ref{sec:experiments}.
