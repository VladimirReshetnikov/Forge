\begin{abstract}
\noindent
The most useful way to build a tactic substantially more capable than \Grind{} is
not to replace its congruence closure or arithmetic engines. It is to let a
proof-producing controller change the shape of the problem: invent a useful
induction motive, generalize a changing accumulator, synthesize a quantified
witness, discover a missing lemma, or request a certificate from a stronger
arithmetic procedure. These operations must cooperate with saturation instead
of running as an unconnected sequence of tactics.

This article specifies \Forge{}, a proposed Lean tactic with a typed
obligation graph, demand-driven structural search, theory-constrained
instantiation, and a strict search/check separation. It identifies concrete
Lean libraries and integration points, gives algorithms and mathematical
justifications, and includes executed Python prototypes. The experiments
produce 215 accepted certificates, replayed without third-party libraries,
and 31 passing test methods. They demonstrate mechanisms and expose several
failure boundaries; they do not establish a performance advantage over Lean.
\end{abstract}
