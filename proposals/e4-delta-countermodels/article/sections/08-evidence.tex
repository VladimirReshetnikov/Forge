\section{Executed experiments and their limits}
\label{sec:evidence}

\subsection{Protocol and retained artifacts}
The tests ran under Python 3.13.5 and SymPy 1.14.0 on the Linux environment
recorded in \code{results/environment.json}. The case generator uses seed
\code{20260915}. Each of 82 corpus cases was run three times in one process;
the certificate bytes, not merely the status, were required to agree between
repetitions. Search and independent replay were separately timed. Imports and
machine-case construction are outside the per-case search timings.

\code{results/corpus.json} contains the exact problems, limits, and returned
certificates or inconclusive reasons. \code{measurements.csv} contains one row
per case. \code{trials.json} preserves individual timings and search statistics.
These observation files are separate because mathematical inputs reject
floating-point JSON values while timing measurements are naturally floating
point.

All 73 certificates replay under
\begin{lstlisting}
python -S replay.py
\end{lstlisting}
with no SymPy, NumPy, SciPy, or Z3 module loaded. The command reports 37 closure
receipts, 28 execution-counterexample receipts, 8 IPC-countermodel receipts,
and 9 cases carrying no conclusion. The largest certificate is 915 bytes in
compact canonical JSON. That number excludes its original problem, metadata,
pretty-printing whitespace, and generated Lean source.

\subsection{Mechanism ablation, not a tactic comparison}
A deliberately narrow comparator, \code{conserved_target_only}, accepts a
single-control-node problem only when every requested target is itself
conserved by every edge and initially zero. It does not synthesize additional
observations or accept finite control flow. It succeeds on 8 of the 37 valid
machine cases, whereas observable closure succeeds on all 37.

This result shows that the test suite distinguishes the new mechanism from
that comparator. It is \emph{not} a measured gain over all previous Forge
prototypes, which include stronger candidate-generation mechanisms, and it is
not a comparison with \code{grind}, Aesop, \code{nlinarith}, or a theorem
prover. The cases were designed to expose specific mechanisms, including
control-indexed relations and hidden auxiliary equalities.

\subsection{Adversarial and differential checks}
The retained pytest run reports \textbf{151 passed}. Its parameterized tests
include all 82 corpus cases; 36 direct certificate/trace/model mutations;
nontransitive and nonmonotone model rejection; invalid numeric encodings;
missing edge and goal coverage; wrong problem reuse; control-flow mismatch;
empty and unreachable cases; resource cutoffs; and an explicit regression for
the seven-sample trap.

Polynomial substitution is tested against SymPy on 200 generated sparse
polynomial instances. The checker expands and evaluates with its own rational
arithmetic, while the reference computes simultaneous symbolic substitution.
Random small formulas and finite frames exercise both bitset and recursive
forcing; those loops also call the actual countermodel checker on each
root-level result. These tests can expose representation and implementation
errors. They do not replace a formal correctness proof of the Python programs.

Every one of the eight named nonderivable IPC cases was found at its expected
smallest world count within the enumerated tree family: one, two, or three
worlds. This is an observation on these cases, not a general minimal-model
algorithm or a promise that a world bound is complete.

\subsection{Timing observations are intentionally secondary}
The median of the machine cases' three-run median search times was about
$2.0$ milliseconds; the corresponding IPC statistic was about $0.045$
milliseconds. These are small in-process synthetic instances, with symbolic
caches potentially warmed, and are not predictions for Lean reification,
subprocess startup, certificate reconstruction, or kernel checking. The raw
per-case values are retained rather than being converted into a headline
speedup claim. The complete experiment driver, excluding initial construction
of the machine case list, took under a second in this run.

\subsection{A failed attempt retained, rather than erased}
The first standalone replay failed before checking any mathematics. Timing
floats had been placed in the same JSON envelope as exact certificate data;
the strict decoder correctly rejected them. The repair separated observation
files from the exact corpus. The policy rejecting mathematical floats was
\emph{not} relaxed. A diagnostic description is retained at
\smallpath{results/diagnostics/initial-replay-failure.txt}, and the successful
replay log is a different file.

This packaging failure is not a rejected mathematical theorem, but recording it
prevents a reader from mistaking every historical run for successful evidence.

\subsection{What these experiments establish, and what they do not}
They establish that the supplied producers and independent replay paths agree
on this explicit corpus; that the new closure mechanism handles the stated
examples; and that specified corruption and semantic-scope tests reject their
invalid inputs. They also provide concrete data for the next Lean integration
step.

They do not establish correctness for every input, a production-grade parser,
a formally verified checker, a dependent Lean reifier, a compiled Forge tactic,
or an advantage on a representative Lean theorem distribution. Neither Lean,
Lake, nor Elan was present in the execution environment. The uncompiled Lean
files cannot fill that evidentiary gap by their mere existence.
