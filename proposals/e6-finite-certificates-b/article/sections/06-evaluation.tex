\section{Executed experiments and what they establish}
\label{sec:experiments}

\subsection{Recorded environment and reproducibility}

The package records one ordered local run with seed \code{20260915}, Python
3.13.5, SymPy 1.14.0, and pytest 9.0.2 on Linux x86-64. Search and independent
replay are timed separately using a monotonic performance counter. The raw
records are in \code{results/experiments.json}; the full original problems and
certificates are in \code{results/certificates.json}. Reproduction writes to a
new directory by default rather than overwriting those records.

The search implementations do not import the checker. They use SymPy expressions
and matrices. The checker uses its own sparse polynomial dictionaries and
standard-library rational arithmetic, without SymPy. A separate invocation
\code{python -S replay.py} accepts all 78 stored artifacts with site packages
disabled. This is implementation diversity at the finite arithmetic boundary,
not a proof that the checker contains no bug.

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}lrrrr@{}}\toprule
Worker & Attempts & Positive certificates & Counterexamples & Unknown \\
\midrule
Inductive ideals & 29 & 27 & 0 & 2 \\
Weighted representations & 48 & 23 & 25 & 0 \\
Binomial telescoping & 5 & 3 & 0 & 2 \\
\bottomrule\end{tabular}
\end{center}

These are counts of different artifact types, not a theorem-proving success
rate. In particular, the twenty-five weighted counterexamples are successful
\emph{inequivalence decisions}, while an ideal unknown is not a counterexample.
The problem families are deliberately constructed to exercise mechanisms and
rejection boundaries; they are not an unbiased sample of Lean developments.

\subsection{Invariant corpus and ablation}

The ideal corpus contains nine named controls and twenty generated curve
families. The generated cases apply random small rational-coordinate changes to
a parameterized quadratic curve and construct polynomial transitions that
preserve it with a nonconstant multiplier. This gives planted, mathematically
known invariants while varying the expanded coefficients and update syntax.
The search is still given the initial map and transition, not the planted
invariant as a basis element.

The restricted ablation solves only global conservation equations
$p(T_e)=p$ together with initial vanishing. It ignores guards as an aid to the
conservation search, so it is deliberately narrower than a full
implication-based invariant method. The important observations are qualitative:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}lrrl@{}}\toprule
Case & Conserved dim. & Inductive dim. & Descent dimensions \\
\midrule
Curve scaling & 0 & 1 & $1,1$ \\
Nonlinear squaring & 0 & 1 & $1,1$ \\
Two transition branches & 0 & 1 & $1,1$ \\
Mutual swap & 1 & 2 & $2,2$ \\
Guarded update & 0 & 1 & $1,1$ \\
Finite orbit, degree two & 1 & 1 & $2,1,1$ \\
Finite orbit, degree one & 0 & 0 & $1,0$ \\
False invariant control & 0 & 0 & $2,1,0$ \\
\bottomrule\end{tabular}
\end{center}

For the mutual-swap case, the one-dimensional conservation space contains
$x+y$, which does not imply the desired $x=0$. The two-dimensional inductive
space contains both $x$ and $y$, and does imply it. This is why comparing only
whether the two spaces are nonempty would miss an important distinction.

The false-invariant control starts at zero and increments the state while
asking for $x=0$. Its empty fixed point is consistent with the false target,
but the worker still reports unknown rather than pretending its restricted
search proved a counterexample. The degree-one finite-orbit miss is a true
target with the same unknown status. Together they enforce the intended result
semantics.

\subsection{Weighted corpus}

The weighted corpus contains the subsequence-count identity, twenty
change-of-basis equivalence pairs of component dimensions two through five,
twenty perturbed pairs that agree on the empty word but disagree later, five
nilpotent-chain examples, and the zero-initial and empty-alphabet edge cases.

For the equivalence pairs, a rational invertible matrix $P$ transforms
\[
 (\alpha,M_a,\beta)\longmapsto
 (\alpha P,P^{-1}M_aP,P^{-1}\beta).
\]
These pairs are equivalent by construction, but their matrices are not
syntactically equal. The perturbations change the final vector in a direction
orthogonal to the initial row, ensuring that a check of only the empty word
cannot distinguish them. Every returned nonempty word is independently
reevaluated.

The five one-letter nilpotent chains of dimensions three through seven have
first distinguishing word lengths two through six, respectively. They attain
the $N-1$ bound and test that the implementation does not cut search short after
several zero outputs. The separate differential tests enumerate all words up
to length $N-1$ in sixty randomly generated representations of dimensions one
through four, comparing the complete finite oracle with the closure search.

\subsection{Telescoping corpus}

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}lrrrrrl@{}}\toprule
Family & $r$ & $a$ & $b$ & Unknowns & Equations & Result \\
\midrule
$\binom nk$ & 1 & 0 & 0 & 3 & 3 & Certificate \\
$\binom nk^2$ & 1 & 1 & 1 & 7 & 9 & Certificate \\
$\binom nk^3$ & 2 & 2 & 5 & 30 & 45 & Certificate \\
$\binom nk^2$, narrow & 1 & 0 & 0 & 3 & 6 & Unknown \\
$\binom nk^3$, order one & 1 & 2 & 2 & 12 & 21 & Unknown \\
\bottomrule\end{tabular}
\end{center}

The successful systems each have nullity one. The two narrow systems have
nullity zero. This demonstrates the importance of recurrence order and
numerator degree, but it does not establish a generally optimal scheduling
policy or a minimal-order theorem for every summand family.

The certificate checker accepts powers one through eight and orders one
through five as data bounds, but only the three named positive families above
were synthesized and recorded. Decoder support for a parameter range must not
be reported as experimental success on every member of that range.

\subsection{Mutation and differential tests}

The test suite reports \textbf{318 passed}. Its decomposition is useful:
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}lr@{}}\toprule
Test class & Count \\
\midrule
Replay of stored original-problem/certificate pairs & 78 \\
Rejection controls, including semantic and input mutations & 148 \\
Tracked-Gr\"obner differential cases & 24 \\
Weighted exhaustive differential cases & 60 \\
Additional direct-sum recurrence regressions & 3 \\
Bounded misses, degree distinction, budgets, import separation & 5 \\
\bottomrule\end{tabular}
\end{center}

The 148 rejections include changing an ideal goal while keeping its old
certificate, altering a transition multiplier, altering initial-span or closure
coordinates, replacing an actual distinguishing word by a nondistinguishing
one, corrupting telescoping numerators or initial values, and introducing a
singular leading coefficient while preserving all algebraic identities. They
also include malformed rationals, booleans used as integers, floating-point
JSON tokens, duplicate object keys, duplicate or unordered polynomial terms,
missing guards, missing letters, and an unsupported summand family.

The twenty-four Gr\"obner cases compare ideal-membership behavior with SymPy's
Gr\"obner implementation and independently expand extracted original-generator
lifts. The search basis need not be reduced, so the test does not incorrectly
require two different normal-form implementations to print identical
remainders. It checks agreement on zero membership and equivalence of
remainders modulo the ideal.

These controls test meaningful failure modes, but the decoder is still a
research implementation. Its byte, term, dimension, exponent, and input-bit
limits are not a complete defense against every expensive intermediate
arithmetic computation. A production worker should enforce wall-clock,
allocation, and total-operation budgets in addition to validating data.

\subsection{Recorded costs, without a tactic-speed claim}

The sum of per-case search times in the one recorded run was approximately
$0.571$ seconds for ideals, $0.421$ seconds for weighted representations, and
$0.319$ seconds for telescoping. The corresponding sums of independent replay
times for returned artifacts were $0.026$, $0.040$, and $0.012$ seconds. These
times exclude process startup and are not repeated-run estimates.

Compact JSON certificate payloads ranged from 134 to 202 bytes for ideal
certificates; from 46 to 887 bytes for weighted artifacts, where many are short
counterexample words; and from 139 to 504 bytes for the three telescopers.
Those sizes exclude the original problem descriptions, pretty-printing, and
future Lean proof terms. The full JSON corpus is larger because it retains
those original problems and readable indentation.

The numbers establish that the small examples and their finite checks are
practical in this environment. They do not predict Gr\"obner performance on
large programs, optimized matrix performance, Lean elaboration time, kernel
checking cost, or success rates against \grind{}, Aesop, LeanHammer, or any
other tactic. None of those head-to-head experiments was performed.

\subsection{The remaining evidence gap}

The executed work establishes implementation behavior for the included Python
fragments, finite certificate replay, and concrete counterexamples to some
restricted precursors. The written proofs establish why the specified
certificates imply their encoded mathematical claims.

The unfilled gap is source formalization: compile the generic Lean soundness
principles, implement proved source bridges, generate actual theorem terms,
and replay them under the pinned Lean/Mathlib environment. The two supplied
Lean files are specimens for that work. Their presence is not evidence that
those steps have already succeeded.
