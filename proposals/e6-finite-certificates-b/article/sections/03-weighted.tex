\section{Worker II: finite residual closure for weighted behaviors}
\label{sec:weighted}

\subsection{Why a recursive equality can be linear algebra}

Consider two programs that fold over a word. Their internal states may be
different, and syntactic rewriting may not find a useful relation between them.
Nevertheless, their observable outputs can have finite-dimensional linear
representations. Once those representations are proved correct, equality for
\emph{every word} becomes a finite closure problem.

A rational weighted representation over a finite alphabet $\Sigma$ consists of
\[
 \alpha\in\Q^{1\times N},\qquad M_a\in\Q^{N\times N}\ (a\in\Sigma),
 \qquad\beta\in\Q^{N\times1}.
\]
Use row vectors throughout. For $w=a_1\cdots a_k$, set
\[
 M_w=M_{a_1}\cdots M_{a_k},\qquad
 f(w)=\alpha M_w\beta,
\]
with $M_\epsilon=I$. This is the usual linear-algebraic view of weighted
automata over a field~\cite{kiefer,tzeng}. The relevant extension to Forge is a
small certificate-producing worker for this fragment, not a new automata
theory.

Two representations can be compared by taking their difference:
\begin{equation}
 \alpha=(\alpha_L\ \alpha_R),\qquad
 M_a=\diag(M^L_a,M^R_a),\qquad
 \beta=\begin{pmatrix}\beta_L\\-\beta_R\end{pmatrix}.
 \label{eq:weighted-difference}
\end{equation}
It suffices to decide whether $\alpha M_w\beta=0$ for all $w$. The dimensions
need not agree, and no syntactic correspondence between their state components
is assumed.

\subsection{Reachable-space search}

Let $\mathcal V=\spanQ\{\alpha M_w:w\in\Sigma^*\}$. This is a subspace of
$\Q^{1\times N}$, so it has dimension at most $N$. A breadth-first worklist
constructs a basis from reachable rows. Each row carries a word that reaches
it.

\par\noindent\begin{minipage}{\linewidth}
\begin{lstlisting}
queue := [(alpha, emptyWord)]
basis := []
while queue is not empty:
    (v, w) := popFront(queue)
    if v * beta != 0:
        return DistinguishingWord(w)
    if v is in span(basis):
        continue
    append v to basis
    for each letter a:
        pushBack(queue, (v * M[a], w ++ [a]))
extract alpha coordinates and transition coordinates in basis
return ClosureCertificate(basis, coordinates)
\end{lstlisting}
\end{minipage}\par

The span test is exact rational elimination. There are at most $N$ independent
rows, and each independent row enqueues at most $|\Sigma|$ successors. Thus at
most $1+|\Sigma|N$ queue rows are examined when no counterexample or budget
cutoff ends the run early. Dependent rows do not need expansion because the
successors of a linear combination are the same linear combinations of the
successors.

\begin{theorem}[Complete finite search over a field]
For a finite-dimensional rational weighted representation and no resource
cutoff, the algorithm returns either a word $w$ with $f(w)\ne0$ or a finite
closure certificate proving $f(w)=0$ for every word.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Each appended row increases the dimension of the current span, so only finitely
many are appended and the queue terminates. Every appended row is reachable.
On termination, the span contains $\alpha$ and is closed under every right
multiplication $M_a$: successors of every basis row were processed and found in
the final span. Induction on word length puts every $\alpha M_w$ in that span.
Every basis row has zero product with $\beta$, so every reachable row does as
well. If the algorithm stops earlier on a nonzero product, its stored word is
an explicit counterexample.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Bounded distinguishing words]
If the represented series is nonzero, some distinguishing word has length at
most $N-1$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Let $V_j$ be the span of rows reached by words of length at most $j$. If
$\alpha=0$, the series is zero. Otherwise $\dim V_0=1$. Whenever
$V_j=V_{j+1}$, that space is closed under every transition and hence contains all
reachable rows. Before stabilization, its dimension increases strictly. It
therefore stabilizes by $j=N-1$. If the output is zero on all rows through that
length, it is zero on their span and thus on all words.
\end{proof}

The proof does not say that brute-force enumeration through length $N-1$ is a
good algorithm: that enumeration is exponential in $N$ for a nontrivial
alphabet. The reachable-space search avoids that expansion by recognizing
linear dependence.

\subsection{The closure certificate}

Let the rows of $B\in\Q^{r\times N}$ span a suitable closed space. Supply a row
$c_0\in\Q^{1\times r}$ and matrices $C_a\in\Q^{r\times r}$ such that
\begin{equation}
 \alpha=c_0B,\qquad BM_a=C_aB\quad(a\in\Sigma),\qquad B\beta=0.
 \label{eq:weighted-cert}
\end{equation}
The replay checks are only matrix equalities over exact rationals.

\begin{theorem}[Weighted-closure soundness]
Equation~\eqref{eq:weighted-cert} implies $\alpha M_w\beta=0$ for all words $w$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Writing $C_w=C_{a_1}\cdots C_{a_k}$, induction on the word gives
\[
 \alpha M_w=c_0C_wB.
\]
The empty case is the initial-coordinate identity. The extension step is
\[
 c_0C_wBM_a=c_0C_wC_aB.
\]
Multiply the resulting equation by $\beta$ and use $B\beta=0$.
\end{proof}

Replay does \emph{not} need a rank computation. The rows of $B$ can be redundant,
and even a larger non-reachable closed space can certify the result if the
three identities hold. Independence is needed only for the efficiency and
termination analysis of the search. Keeping it out of the certificate checker
makes the proof principle smaller.

The zero-initial-vector case admits $r=0$: the initial-coordinate equality then
forces $\alpha=0$, and the closure and output conditions are empty. The empty
alphabet case has just the empty word and requires no transition identities.
Both cases are covered by the executed tests. Matrix dimensions and the exact
alphabet inventory are checked explicitly, so omitting a difficult letter is
not an allowed certificate simplification.

A counterexample certificate is smaller still: it contains a word, and replay
computes its nonzero output from the original representation. This is a genuine
negative answer in the encoded fragment. It transfers to a source program only
after the source-to-representation bridge is proved, just as a positive answer
does.

\subsection{Building representations from code}

\paragraph{Linear folds.}
If a state update is $v\mapsto vM_a$ and the output is $v\beta$, the
representation is direct. The front end must prove the equations connecting
these coordinates to the source fold. It should not infer those equations from
the shape of pretty-printed code.

\paragraph{Affine folds.}
For $v\mapsto vA_a+b_a$, adjoin a constant coordinate and represent the state as
$z=(1,v)$. Then
\[
 z\longmapsto z\begin{pmatrix}1&b_a\\0&A_a\end{pmatrix}.
\]
The invariant that the first coordinate is one follows from the matrix form.
Affine outputs are handled by the same coordinate. This covers many counters,
linear accumulators, and statistics of words.

\paragraph{Scalar recurrences.}
A constant-coefficient linear recurrence has a companion-matrix state and a
one-letter alphabet. Equivalence of two recurrence implementations is therefore
an instance of the same worker. Polynomial coefficients depending on the index
are not constant matrices; either a proved finite lift exists or another
worker, such as telescoping, is required.

\paragraph{Addition and products of observations.}
Direct sums represent addition. For pointwise products over $\Q$,
\begin{align*}
 \alpha_{fg}&=\alpha_f\otimes\alpha_g,\\
 M^{fg}_a&=M^f_a\otimes M^g_a,\\
 \beta_{fg}&=\beta_f\otimes\beta_g.
\end{align*}
The Kronecker-product identity gives
$f(w)g(w)=\alpha_{fg}M^{fg}_w\beta_{fg}$. This permits nonlinear \emph{output}
identities even though the expanded state dynamics remain linear. Repeated
tensor lifting can multiply dimensions rapidly, so it requires an explicit
state-dimension budget and subsequent reachable-space reduction.

\paragraph{Finite observations of an infinite alphabet.}
A source type need not literally be finite if a proved classifier maps each
input element to one of finitely many transition classes and the update depends
only on that classifier. The bridge theorem must establish this factoring.
Without it, checking a finite sample of letters does not establish a word
identity over an infinite alphabet.

\subsection{Worked example: a product of counts versus subsequence counts}

For a binary word, let $A,B$ count occurrences of $a,b$, and let $AB,BA$ count
ordered subsequences of length two. The row state is
\[
 z=(1,A,B,AB,BA).
\]
Appending $a$ updates $A$ to $A+1$ and $BA$ to $BA+B$; appending $b$ updates $B$
to $B+1$ and $AB$ to $AB+A$. All other coordinates remain unchanged. Starting
from $(1,0,0,0,0)$, the output $AB+BA$ is therefore a five-dimensional weighted
representation.

A two-dimensional affine counter represents $A$, and another represents $B$.
Their tensor product has four coordinates and represents $AB$ as the
\emph{product of counts}. To avoid a notational ambiguity in an implementation,
call that output $A\cdot B$, reserving \code{countABSubseq} for the subsequence
counter.

The difference construction has ambient dimension nine. The executed search
finds five reachable basis rows, reached by
\[
 \epsilon,\quad a,\quad b,\quad ab,\quad ba,
\]
and examines eleven worklist rows. Its certificate verifies
\[
 \forall w\in\{a,b\}^*,\qquad
 \operatorname{countABSubseq}(w)+\operatorname{countBASubseq}(w)
 =\operatorname{countA}(w)\operatorname{countB}(w)
\]
for the encoded folds. A separate simple combinatorial proof pairs every
$a$-position with every $b$-position and partitions the pairs by their order.
That proof explains the identity, but it is \emph{not} supplied to the search.
The prototype derives the finite linear relation instead.

The example exercises several capabilities at once: different source states,
noncommuting letter matrices, a tensor-composed right side, and an invariant
space smaller than the combined coordinate space. Replacing both sides by a
single known algebraic expression would not test those capabilities.

\subsection{Counterexamples, minimization, and use during synthesis}

A minimal state representation is not required to decide zero equivalence.
It can nonetheless be useful after success. First restrict to the reachable
space using $B$ and $C_a$. Then compute which residual directions affect future
outputs, quotienting only after proving the corresponding observation map.
Classical weighted-automaton minimization provides the algebraic background
for this second phase~\cite{kiefer}. The prototype implements reachable-space
closure, not full minimal realization.

During term synthesis, an exact distinguishing word can reject a candidate
whose proved weighted summary disagrees with the requested weighted
specification. A proved match can discharge \emph{that observable contract}.
Neither result justifies replacing equality of arbitrary source functions by
equality of their lengths or counts. For example, two list-valued functions can
have equal length on all inputs while returning different elements in a
different order. The result type of the adapter should name the observable it
has established.

This provides a useful bridge to Leant and Djex: their current semantic
foundation already associates behavioral analyses with source-owned typed
candidate graphs~\cite{djex-semantic}. A weighted domain can analyze a new class
of behaviors without altering the candidate's type certificate or claiming
that the existing length abstraction suddenly became complete.

\subsection{Cost model and boundaries}

With incremental rational elimination, each independence test can be reduced to
$O(N^2)$ field operations, and each dense successor multiplication also costs
$O(N^2)$. Across $O(|\Sigma|N)$ generated rows, a straightforward optimized
implementation has an $O(|\Sigma|N^3)$ field-operation bound. Bit complexity
also depends on coefficient growth. Fraction-free or modular linear algebra
with exact reconstruction is a production option; reconstruction must still
pass the finite equalities in Equation~\eqref{eq:weighted-cert}.

The included SymPy implementation recomputes linear solves for span membership
and coordinate extraction. It is intentionally simpler than an incremental
cubic implementation, so its measured times must not be presented as evidence
for that optimized bound. Its role is to validate the algorithm, certificate,
and source examples.

The field assumption matters for the decision algorithm. Rational weighted
zero equivalence is not Boolean-language equivalence with the word ``rational''
erased, and it is not arbitrary-semiring equivalence. A nondeterministic
weighted representation may count accepting paths rather than indicate their
existence. A Lean bridge must preserve which semantics is being modeled.
