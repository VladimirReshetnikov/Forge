and polynomial factors into small certificates whose analytic content is a
single integrating-factor lemma. Add a total eventual-sign procedure that
constructs explicit rational cutoffs rather than merely asserting domination.

\textbf{Principal results}\par
A nondecreasing factor order dominates every permutation of the same differential
factors. Extra factors form a monotone hierarchy of stronger positivity
certificates. An interior-zero obstruction identifies when that hierarchy
cannot help. Explicit tail bounds complement the ray certificates.
\vfill
\textbf{Evidence boundary}\par
284 separately bound certificate receipts replay under standard-library-only
Python; 18 unit-test methods pass. The factor-order ablation checks 14,409
permutations on 200 inputs. Lean bridge and replay sources are supplied as
\emph{uncompiled candidates}; this archive does not contain an implemented
\forge{} tactic or a comparison against \code{grind}.

\begin{abstract}
The existing Forge design already contains extensive polynomial arithmetic,
structural induction, witness synthesis, and finite certificates for unbounded
discrete behaviour. This article develops a complementary analytic capability:
exact sign certificates for rational exponential polynomials and for source
expressions reducible to them by proved changes of variable.

The central object is a differential ladder
$q_{j+1}=(\D-\rho_j)q_j$ with a zero terminal function and nonnegative initial
values. Its soundness follows from an integrating factor. A swapping argument
eliminates factorial search over factor order: ascending order succeeds whenever
any ordering of the same multiset can succeed. Additional factors enlarge the
accepted cone, but a strict-positivity theorem shows that nonzero ladders cannot
certify an interior zero. This gives a mathematical routing criterion rather
than another budget heuristic. A second worker computes a certified eventual
sign and explicit cutoff for every nonzero member of the exact fragment.

The article supplies proofs, sparse representations, certificate formats,
source-translation obligations, an implementation sequence, reproducible
experiments, adversarial checks, and candidate Lean proof emission. It separates
new algorithms from existing libraries to reuse, and mathematical guarantees
from the narrower properties actually established by the accompanying code.
\end{abstract}
