\section{Executed experiments and what they establish}
\label{sec:experiments}

\subsection{Environment, inputs, and receipts}

The experiments ran with Python 3.13.5 on Linux x86-64, using the standard
library only. Commands were invoked with \code{python -S}, which disables
site-package initialisation. The seed for the recorded experiment bundle is
\code{20260915}. Full interpreter and platform strings are retained in
\pathline{results/experiment-summary.json}.

The original canonical problems and requested goal types are stored in
\pathline{results/problems.json}.
The separate receipt stream in
\pathline{results/certificates.json}
references those problems by identifier. The replay program reopens both files
and calls \code{verify_goal}; it does not trust a polynomial or domain supplied
only by the receipt. All 284 stored receipts are accepted against their recorded
requests.

This total is internal to one bundle. It is not added to the eighteen older
Forge runs. Moreover, some receipts intentionally concern the same function
with different domains or result types. They are not independent discoveries
or a solved-goal percentage on a theorem corpus.

The eight named cases include the reflected exponential tangent remainder,
quadratic and cubic hyperbolic remainders, both entropy charts, the two
logarithmic bounds, and a damped positive square. The random eventual-sign
generator creates canonical sums from eight random monomial proposals, rates
with denominator three, degrees below six, and small signed integer
coefficients. It tests the total tail constructor on varied exact inputs;
it is not sampled evidence of the universal theorem, whose proof is in
Section~\ref{sec:tails}.

\subsection{An ablation with an exhaustive finite oracle}

The most informative algorithmic ablation tests the factor-order theorem.
For each of 200 independently generated inputs with rates drawn from
$\{-2,1,3\}$ and degrees at most one per block, the experiment enumerates every
distinct permutation of the native multiset. Across the cases it checks 14,409
orders. Forty-four inputs have an accepted ascending ladder, and in every case
\[
 \text{ascending ladder accepts}
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \text{some enumerated order accepts}.
\]
There are zero mismatches. The records retain the number of accepting orders
per case, so the ablation is reproducible rather than merely an assertion that
both code paths ``usually agree.''

This checks a finite consequence of Theorem~\ref{thm:order} against exhaustive
search. It does not replace the proof and it does not establish that every
positive input among the other 156 cases lacks some richer certificate. The
counterpart in the unit tests uses two rates and a separate seed.

\subsection{Independent calculations and mutation tests}

The test suite contains 18 test methods. Several methods contain hundreds of
cases; the method count is not the case count. Important checks are:

\paragraph{Differentiation through three representations.}
Two hundred random inputs are differentiated through six random factors.
The producer's termwise loop is compared against the checker's coefficient
stencil. Initial values are also compared with a direct jet oracle:
\[
 \D^m(x^ke^{\lambda x})\big|_{x=0}
 =\begin{cases}
 \dfrac{m!}{(m-k)!}\lambda^{m-k},&m\ge k,\\
 0,&m<k.
 \end{cases}
\]
The oracle multiplies the scalar characteristic polynomials
$\prod_j(z-\rho_j)$ and applies their coefficients to these ordinary jets.
It does not generate the same ladder by calling the producer again.

\paragraph{Annihilation and tail checks.}
One hundred random native annihilators are checked to kill their inputs.
Three hundred additional random tail receipts and the zero input are replayed
in the unit suite. These cases are separate from the 240 retained random tail
records; their counts are not pooled into an inflated certificate count.

\paragraph{Enclosure diagnostics.}
Two hundred rational exponential arguments are compared against
\code{Decimal.exp} at 160-digit precision. This is a high-precision diagnostic
oracle, not a proof of the enclosure theorem. Precision-refinement cases,
negative arguments, zero arguments, and resource-parameter validation are also
exercised. The mathematical enclosure proof is
\eqref{eq:exp-bounds} and directed rounding.

\paragraph{Corruption and source substitution.}
Tests change a differential factor, an initial lower bound, a polynomial
coefficient, a terminal length, a source polynomial, an anchor, a sign, a
cutoff, a tail order, an interval endpoint, and a split point. They also try
floating-point and Boolean values where exact numbers are required. The
specified corruptions are rejected. The tests expressly reject using an
anchor-one proof at anchor zero, a tail as a whole-ray proof, and a negative
point as a successful nonnegativity proof.

These tests are evidence of the tested invariants. They are not a security
audit of a hostile-input service. Input-size, coefficient-bit-length, recursion,
and allocation limits still need production engineering.

\subsection{Unknown results retained deliberately}

Two instructive misses are retained in the summary. The interior-zero case
$(e^x-2)^2$ remains unknown under the bounded auxiliary-factor search; its
stronger all-factor obstruction is proved in Theorem~\ref{thm:strict}. The
natural-interval attempt on $e^x-1-x$ over $[0,1]$ remains unknown at subdivision
depth 14; the ladder certifies the same nonnegativity statement directly.

These misses are not hidden by counting only returned receipts. Conversely,
an unknown result is never recorded as falsity of the target. A failed local
certificate search and a checked negative point are different outcomes in both
the experiment harness and the proposed worker API.

\subsection{Replay separation and its limits}

The replay process checks that the search module is not imported. It verifies
stored receipts without regenerating them. Derivative replay uses a distinct
calculation, and tail replay checks sufficient inequalities rather than
recomputing the proposed cutoff.

There is an important shared component: point and interval enclosure arithmetic
is used by both the corresponding searches and replay. A common error there
could survive producer/checker agreement. The independent high-precision
checks and elementary mathematical proof reduce that risk but do not eliminate
it. A Lean soundness theorem for that component, or replacement by a proved
library adapter, is the intended resolution.

The stored certificate JSON contributes 154,227 bytes when each individual
receipt is serialised compactly and their sizes are summed. This excludes
problem data, pretty-printing whitespace, indices, and other files. The largest
single receipt in that measure is 14,694 bytes. These figures characterise the
reference format, not compiled Lean proof sizes or memory consumption.

A single elapsed time is retained as environment metadata, but no timing claim
is made against another prover or another backend. No Lean compilation,
\code{grind} comparison, or corpus success-rate evaluation was performed in
this environment.
