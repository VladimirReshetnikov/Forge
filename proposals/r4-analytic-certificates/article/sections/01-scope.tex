\section{The gap this extension addresses}
\label{sec:scope}

\subsection{A delta against the repository, not another Forge introduction}

The audited Forge snapshot is
\pathline{c98e47c5f804e92880fc1d0e378c1b95832b685c}.
Its merged article already treats polynomial cones, exact quadratic decomposition,
Bernstein and univariate certificates, structural generalisation, witness
construction, discrete observable-space and ideal closure, finite-algebra
covers, telescoping, and operator transport. Its status ledger distinguishes
executed Python prototypes, emitted Lean candidates, and unimplemented runtime
components. Those mechanisms are prerequisites here, not contributions to be
reintroduced under new names~\cite{forge-readme,forge-status,forge-nonlinear,forge-closure}.

The narrow addition is \emph{analytic sign information}. For example, replacing
$e^x$ by a fresh positive real variable discards precisely the relationship to
$x$ needed to establish
\[
 e^x\ge 1+x,\qquad
 e^x\ge \sum_{k=0}^{n}\frac{x^k}{k!}\quad(x\ge0),\qquad
 \exists X\ \forall x\ge X:\ e^x>1000x^5.
\]
Polynomial reasoning alone does not recover that relationship from an opaque
atom. It needs a proved analytic consequence, a derivative identity, or a
change of variable which exposes the missing structure.

This article does not assert that existing Lean tactics fail on these displayed
examples. Several have short library proofs. Their purpose is to exhibit the
input language and proof certificates, not to construct a weak baseline. The
proposed capability is a systematic worker for expanded, unfamiliar combinations
of such terms, including automatic cutoffs and useful routing diagnostics.

The audit read the merged repository's overview, status, nonlinear arithmetic,
closure, and conclusion material, and examined neighbouring indexing diagnostics.
It was not a line-by-line review of every historical proposal. Consequently the
claim is a substantive delta against those documented capabilities, not a claim
that no related word or idea appears anywhere in the repository.

\subsection{What Leant and Djex contribute to this design}

The inspected neighbouring snapshots are Leant
\pathline{6bf05ad78c467989e68290f2d08bbed40802d485}
and Djex
\pathline{e8778f4ebd63e1f9b9b410fa4de8d14a8a04c9e5}.
Their current indexing diagnostics make a useful distinction: synthesising the
right components separately does not establish that the original bounded query
has been solved. The original integer-indexing acceptance remains absent in
those diagnostics~\cite{leant,djex}.

There is an exact analytic analogue. A certificate for $f(x)>0$ when $x\ge32$
does not close a request for $f(x)\ge0$ when $x\ge0$. A proof about the transformed
exponential polynomial does not close a source theorem until the logarithmic
chart and its domain have been proved. A generated nonnegativity theorem does
not close a strict-positivity request. Accordingly the prototype has a separate
\code{verify_goal} layer, not merely a predicate saying that a receipt is
internally consistent. These particular distinctions are enforced by negative
tests, rather than being left as aspirations in a general trust model.

\subsection{Mathematical provenance and the intended claim}

The ingredients are classical: constant-coefficient differential equations,
integrating factors, real exponential-polynomial function spaces, and exact
Taylor bounds. Extended Chebyshev systems provide a broader setting for related
ordered bases~\cite{chebyshev}; differential-invariant proof systems address a
much larger class of continuous dynamics~\cite{differential-invariance}.
Nothing here claims invention of differential induction or of positive bases.

The contribution relative to Forge is the particular algorithmic package: a
small accepted language, an ordering theorem that removes an apparent search
dimension, an exact enrichment hierarchy, a useful impossibility criterion for
that hierarchy, a total explicit tail constructor, and detailed lowering to
ordinary Lean theorems. The proofs are included so that these claims need not
rest on an analogy with a larger literature.
