\section*{Abstract}
\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{Abstract}
Forge already proposes a substantial collection of structural, algebraic, and
finite-closure workers. The next useful contribution should therefore be a new
class of obligations, rather than another description of its controller. This
article develops three such classes: reachability and safety of systems with an
unbounded stack; synthesis of a strategy that meets an obligation infinitely
often against an adversary; and exact absorption probabilities and expected
costs of rational finite-state probabilistic programs.

Each class receives an explicit finite certificate language, a positive and a
negative outcome, a terminating search algorithm on a stated fragment, and a
separate replay computation. Pushdown reachability uses productive pop-summary
DAGs, while nonreachability uses closed exclusion relations. B\"uchi games use
positional policies with either a rank that resets at accepting vertices or a
co-B\"uchi rank that bounds the total number of accepting visits. Markov chains
use exact Bellman residuals together with a properness potential; a positive
path into a closed nonterminal class certifies failure of almost-sure
termination. The mathematical kernels are classical. The contribution is their
precise packaging as new Forge capabilities, including source bridges that
preserve stack suffixes, ownership and temporal progress, or probability mass.

A dependency-free Python package implements all three workers. Its experiments
include 881 pushdown cases, 22,300 game arenas, and 770 Markov chains. All 28,566
stored certificate records replay without importing a search module. The game
suite exhausts every total arena with at most three vertices, every ownership
map, and every accepting set. A 31-node pushdown certificate describes a run of
$2,147,483,647$ steps without expanding it. These are prototype results, not Lean
proofs or evidence of an advantage over another tactic. The article gives
soundness arguments, concrete Lean module contracts, implementation priorities,
and explicit acceptance gates for closing that remaining gap.

\section*{How to read this package}
The mathematical proposal is in Sections~\ref{sec:delta}--\ref{sec:bridges}.
Implementation contracts and libraries follow in Section~\ref{sec:implementation}.
The measurements and their units are in Section~\ref{sec:experiments}.
Appendices describe the wire format and reproduction commands. The accompanying
Python files, rather than pseudocode in this article, are the executable
reference. Read \code{results/summary.json} for the machine-readable evidence
and \code{docs/STATUS.md} before interpreting any result as a formal proof.
