\begin{abstract}
Forge's present design already has an extensive proof-planning architecture. The most useful next contribution is therefore not another scheduler, another generalization heuristic, or another statement of the search/checking boundary. This report develops three additional capability areas through four executable producers. First, a goal-generated pullback algorithm constructs a finite vector of polynomial invariants whose entries may change together. Second, ideal closure replaces constant linear dependence with polynomial dependence, resolving nonlinear cases in which the vector-space algorithm necessarily keeps growing. The ideal worker retains original-goal provenance, obtains explicit multiplier certificates, and has an unbounded termination argument for a precisely stated unguarded polynomial fragment. Third, finite-state relational certificates establish equality of fold observations for every finite input word, or produce a shortest distinguishing word. Fourth, a restricted creative-telescoping worker discovers recurrences for binomial-power moments with moving support and explicit endpoint reasoning.

All four producers were executed. A separate standard-library-only checker replays their sparse polynomial identities, finite relations, and concrete counterexamples without importing SymPy. The recorded run contains 178 positive certificates, 135 counterexample certificates, and four resource-bounded unknown outcomes; 240 finite-state results agree with an independent greatest-fixed-point oracle. The report supplies complete mathematical arguments for the certificate rules, concrete integration interfaces, a dependency plan, and adversarial tests. It makes no measured claim against \texttt{grind} or any other Lean tactic. The algorithms build on established algebraic invariant analysis, automata equivalence, and creative telescoping; the contribution is their focused adaptation and executable extension of the reviewed Forge design, not a claim to have invented those fields.
\end{abstract}
