{\large\scshape A concrete extension to the Forge research program\par}
\vspace{0.55in}
{\Huge\bfseries Forge with\par Certified Symmetry\par}
\vspace{0.25in}
{\Large Stabilizer chains, canonical images,\par symbolic orbit counting, and algebraic reduction\par}
\vspace{0.5in}
{\large Prepared for Vladimir Reshetnikov\par}
{\large September 15, 2026\par}
\vspace{0.5in}
\begin{abstract}
Forge already proposes a wide range of certificate-producing mathematical workers. This article adds a coherent new capability: reasoning with a finite permutation group through a small, exact certificate, then reusing that certificate to canonicalize configurations, count orbits, and reduce symmetric algebraic searches. The principal object is a complete stabilizer chain whose strong generators retain straight-line provenance in the original generators. Its checker proves enough local facts to justify both positive and negative membership answers, an exact order, and a unique coset normal form. A separate coset-cover certificate establishes canonical images; recognized symmetric and alternating groups admit substantially cheaper image and counting certificates. A sparse Reynolds-projection certificate connects the same machinery to polynomial reasoning without enumerating the group.

The accompanying standard-library Python implementation was executed, with independent oracle comparisons, malformed-input controls, and search-free replay. It checked a certificate for $\Sym_{40}$ using 820 transversal entries, not $40!$ explicit permutations. These are Python experiments and mathematical soundness arguments, not Lean-kernel proofs. The article identifies the exact reification and formalization obligations needed to make the extension part of a real \code{forge} tactic.
\end{abstract}
