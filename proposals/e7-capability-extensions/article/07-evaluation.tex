\section{Executed experiments and their evidential limits}
\label{sec:experiments}

\subsection{Reproducibility envelope}

The recorded run used Python 3.13.5 on Linux. The main experiments and standalone
replay have no third-party runtime dependency. Their seeds are fixed and their
inputs are serialized beside the certificates. The optional algebra comparison
used SymPy 1.14.0 with a separate seed. The complete scripts and outputs are in
\code{prototype/} and \code{results/}.

The main run finished in about 1.8 seconds in this environment. That elapsed
time is only a reproducibility observation for these small fixtures. It excludes
Lean elaboration, source extraction, solver startup in a production worker, and
kernel checking. The algorithmic counts below are more meaningful than ratios
of sub-millisecond timings.

The distractor ordering is deliberately unfavorable and held fixed across both

contracts, not robustness under all candidate orders. No native Djex or Leant
execution was performed in this study.

The Gr\"obner comparison is important because many positive ideal fixtures need
only one generator. Such examples alone exercise polynomial division but barely
exercise completion. The separate random two-variable corpus contains two or
three quadratic generators and tests both original-generator representations and
normal remainders against another implementation.

The high-degree negative control is also meaningful: degree-one linear-space
closure does \emph{not} incorrectly prove stability of $x$ under $x'=x^2$.
The ideal lane then succeeds legitimately with a polynomial multiplier. This
checks the intended handoff between algorithms rather than just two unrelated
success lists.

\subsection{Independent replay and deliberate corruptions}

The standalone replayer accepted 135 encoded certificate records: 113
constant-action invariant records, 14 ideal-preservation records, four ranking
records, one indexing-algebra record, and three orbit-counterexample records.
An affine record with an empty invariant space and zero target is a valid
consistency check, not a nontrivial solved theorem. The count therefore must not
be described as ``135 Lean theorems proved.''

The replayer contains its own polynomial arithmetic and substitution routines;
it imports none of the search modules. It checks identities and inequality
combinations rather than rediscovering the candidate. It was also run using
\code{python -S}, which disables normal site initialization and demonstrates that
no installed symbolic-algebra package is needed for replay. This remains a
Python research checker, not a formally verified implementation or a hardened
hostile-input verifier.

Thirty-one named corruptions were rejected. They include wrong action matrices,
missing transitions, changed initializations, a fabricated target combination,
floating-point and noncanonical rational encodings, omitted call edges, erased
phases, wrong lexicographic pivots, missing natural-domain proofs, captured
binders, an outer-index escape, a wrong predecessor, changed Church semantics,
wrong ideal multipliers, and reversed noncommuting execution order.

The portable regression suite has 29 test methods and passes. Some methods
replay the already counted certificates, so these numbers overlap; there is no
combined ``total benchmark cases'' statistic. The optional SymPy comparison is
separate from both the portable tests and the acceptance boundary.

\subsection{What remains unmeasured}

No claim is made here about Mathlib-wide coverage, a new default tactic's latency,
Lean proof-term sizes, kernel replay times, or a solved original Leant behavior
cell. The source-level folds, cyclic proof constructors, and polynomial maps in
these experiments were supplied or generated in the prototype's own language.
Extracting them from arbitrary Lean goals remains implementation work.

These limitations do not make the experiments merely illustrative. They test
specific algorithmic claims: a greatest stable subspace, exact target-driven
ideal completion, compositional algebra synthesis, and sound descent certificates.
They establish enough of each mechanism to make its next integration gate
precise and falsifiable.

\section{Conclusion}

The most valuable extension to Forge is not a longer list of fallback tactics.
It is the ability to discover and certify \emph{interfaces between partial
solutions}. A continuation carrier lets a fold's step be synthesized as its own
problem. An inductive equation family lets an arithmetic proof use mutually
supporting facts rather than demand individual conservation. A rank on a tagged
obligation graph lets recursive proof structure be turned into ordinary
well-founded induction.

The proposed design deliberately uses two algebraic lanes. The linear lane is
predictable, degree-bounded, and complete in its affine fragment. The ideal lane
is target-directed and handles polynomial multipliers and degree growth, at the
cost of potentially expensive algebra. Cyclic compilation then consumes their
proved local facts without accepting a circular argument.

The immediate implementation priority is to lower the already executable exact
certificates to Lean, not to enlarge the untrusted search first. Once that gate
works, the continuation and cyclic extensions have sharply defined entry points,
concrete regression targets, and evidence that their central algorithms behave
as specified.
