\begin{abstract}
Forge already proposes structural proof planning, arithmetic witnesses, finite closure certificates, nonlinear polynomial reasoning, and several external-solver interfaces. This article does not propose those mechanisms again. It develops an analytic lane for statements involving real exponentials and hyperbolic functions, with three concrete certificate families.

The first family is an anchored cofactor ladder: repeated application of $\D-c$ transforms an exponential polynomial into an ordinary polynomial with nonnegative coefficients, while every intermediate value at zero is nonnegative. A scalar integrating-factor theorem proves soundness. An adjacent-exchange argument proves that increasing cofactor order is sufficient for feasibility within an input-spectrum grammar. A multiplicity argument then reduces shortest-certificate construction to the number of zero cofactors, a monotone one-dimensional feasibility problem. The prototype implements this deterministic compiler and a checkable minimality receipt.

The second family certifies coupled residuals through a positive differential system with a rational Metzler matrix. The third supplies exact rational enclosures, point counterexamples, and root brackets whose derivative-sign covers prove uniqueness in the stated interval. These workers have deliberately different logical outputs.

The recorded corpus contains 196 positive ladder cases, 40 coupled-system cases, 44 negative-point cases, 8 true functions rejected by the ladder grammar, and 16 isolated-root cases. The optimal compiler reduces the total number of cofactor steps from 1,212 to 288 on the same 196 positive inputs. All 624 targeted invalid mutations are rejected. Finite independent checks cover 200 random small permutation problems and 200 symbolic derivatives. These are research-code results, not Lean-kernel proofs and not evidence of superiority over any existing tactic. The article gives the mathematical proofs, algorithms, concrete library targets, source-to-goal obligations, limitations, and acceptance gates needed to turn the lane into a genuine Forge extension.
\end{abstract}
