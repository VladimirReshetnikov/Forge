\section{Executed evidence and its limits}
\label{sec:evidence}

\subsection{What was run}
The delivered implementation uses only the Python standard library and was run
under Python 3.13.5 on Linux. The test command uses \code{python -S}, disabling
site-package initialization. No SMT solver, numerical library, or external
model checker was installed or used. The original corpus and its exact models,
receipts, oracle outcomes, and recorded timings are in \code{results/}.
The generator seed is \code{20260915}.

There are three different units of evidence. The unit suite has 27 test methods,
some containing many assertions. The randomized suite contains 640 model or
model-pair instances. The stored replay corpus has those 640 instances plus ten
handcrafted examples, for 650 certificate objects. The mutation suite makes two
invalid edits to each of those 650 objects, for 1,300 rejection checks. Those
numbers should not be added into a single success count.

``Positive'' means safe for the counter and mixed workers, and equivalent for
the equality worker. ``Negative'' means unsafe or inequivalent, with an
executable witness. No randomized case returned unknown at the configured
limits. That is a property of these small generated corpora, not a general
success-rate prediction.

For all 37 inequivalent randomized equality pairs, the returned word length
also agreed with the exhaustive oracle's shortest mismatch length. The other
workers do not claim shortest witnesses. In particular, the common-parameter
construction may choose a larger initial population than necessary.

\subsection{What the differential oracles actually do}
The random counter generator conserves total token mass and uses a fixed initial
marking. The oracle exhausts the finite reachable concrete graph; it does not
truncate counters at an arbitrary chosen value. Because the state space is
finite for these inputs, its negative search result is a complete result for
the generated model, not a depth-bounded observation.

The equality oracle performs concrete paired-state BFS over an atom carrier
of size $k_A+k_B+c+1$. Corollary~\ref{cor:cutoff} justifies that carrier for the
admitted machines. It does not build a quotient or use the producer's canonical
forms. The random mixed oracle similarly combines conservation of token mass
with a derived atom carrier of size $k+c+1$.

These are algorithmically different oracles, but not wholly independent
implementations of every layer: they reuse the checker's concrete evaluator.
The certificate checker itself does not import the producers and independently
implements arithmetic predecessor checks and nominal interpretation. Thus the
evidence detects many producer mistakes, including quotient and worklist
mistakes, but a semantic bug shared by the checker and oracle can survive their
agreement. The paper proofs and targeted tests reduce that risk; only a
formalized soundness theorem would close the logical gap.

\subsection{Workload size and construction bias}
Across the randomized counter corpus the oracle visits 537 states; across the
nominal corpus it visits 1,097, compared with 440 producer-reported orbit visits;
across the mixed corpus it visits 479. These are intentionally tiny systems,
often settled at or near the initial state. They are suitable regression and
differential tests, not scalability evidence. The aggregate orbit/concrete
counts also do not justify a speedup ratio: exploration can stop at different
points, and the operations have different costs.

Nominal pairs are built from a random source machine and a register permutation.
On alternating cases one output expression is changed. Some changes are on
unreachable or observationally irrelevant branches, which is why the count of
inequivalent pairs is less than half the corpus. This construction supplies
both semantic positives and negatives, but is biased toward closely related
machines. It is not an unbiased sample of program equivalence obligations.

Recorded single-run times for the randomized loops, including their oracle
comparisons, are approximately 0.068 seconds, 0.159 seconds, and 0.076 seconds
for the counter, nominal, and mixed families respectively. These values merely
show that the experiments executed; there is no timing distribution, warm-up
control, Lean elaboration cost, or comparison with a tactic. Reproduction on
another interpreter may change the timings without changing any certificate.

\subsection{Mutations and positive robustness controls}
The 1,300 corpus mutations are primarily \emph{structural or required-content}
checks: an invalid schema version, followed by a missing mandatory state/basis,
an out-of-range initial-family index, or an empty mismatch word. Their complete
rejection does not establish resistance to every semantic forgery or hostile
input. The count should not be marketed as 1,300 deep independent attacks.

The unit suite adds targeted semantic controls. It exhausts 625 small scalar
instances of the exact predecessor identity and checks 64 scalar initial-family
separation instances. It tests enabling versus net change, a backward-closure
failure even when target inclusion and initial separation pass, insufficient
fresh-name alphabets, fixed constants, empty versus atom zero, source-model
mutation, lost orbit states, truncated witnesses, and unknown budget outcomes.
It also rejects malformed numeric values, wrong vector dimensions, unsupported
atom operations, duplicate JSON keys, and nonfinite JSON constants.

Positive robustness controls matter too. A safe basis extended with redundant
or dominated vectors is accepted when it still satisfies the obligations.
Renaming every nonconstant atom label in an equivalence certificate to sparse,
noncanonical integers is also accepted. A checker that rejects every edited
receipt may simply be checking agreement with its producer, not the intended
mathematical property.

\subsection{Replay separation and remaining risks}
The standalone replay command imports only the checker and standard-library
modules; it asserts that the producer and oracle modules are absent from
\code{sys.modules}. It accepts all 650 stored objects, distributed as 305
counter, 163 nominal, and 182 mixed objects. The checker's fresh representative
is chosen differently from the producer's, and its equality pattern is a matrix
rather than the producer's scan-normal form.

These facts establish operational separation, not a trusted computing base.
Python, the parser, the arithmetic implementation, the evaluator, and the
checker remain unverified. The JSON loader limits total text size and rejects
some malformed encodings, but it is not hardened against every adversarial
resource-exhaustion pattern. In particular, large lists, huge integers,
pathological expression trees, and expensive receipts need additional
allocation, node-count, and bit-length budgets before use as an unattended
service.

No source reifier, Lean checker, or all-input Lean theorem was executed here.
The small Lean specimen has status \code{NOT\_RUN}. The baseline repository's
previous Lean elaborations remain its own evidence and are not attributed to
these new workers. The delivered results justify implementing and evaluating
the formal workers; they do not establish that Forge has already gained any
end-to-end theorem-solving capability.
